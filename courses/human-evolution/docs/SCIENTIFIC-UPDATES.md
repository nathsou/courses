# Scientific revisions for the human evolution course

This targeted review identifies changes the interactive course should make to its historical source account. Hublin's lectures were delivered in 2017, following several studies published in 2016 or earlier; the symposium dates from 2018. Primary research available by 4 October 2026 supports the revisions below. The proposed teaching decisions are editorial interpretations of that evidence, not claims that every debate has been resolved.

Use these changes in the main course text. Where the optional YouTube references expose an older account, explain the difference and cite the newer paper. Automatic captions are useful for locating a claim but can misstate names, isotope labels and numbers. The transcript archive preserves them unchanged.

## Revisions supported by later primary research

| Topic and chapters | Evidence to incorporate | Course decision and limits |

| Menopause and post-reproductive survival, chapters 2–3 | [Wood et al., Science, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10645439/) report demographic and hormonal evidence of menopause in wild chimpanzees at Ngogo, Uganda. | Correct any categorical statement that wild chimpanzees do not experience substantial post-reproductive survival. Identify this as evidence from one community; it neither establishes a universal chimpanzee pattern nor by itself resolves the evolutionary explanation for human menopause. Keep grandmother and other hypotheses distinct from observations. |
| Neonatal development, chapters 5–6 | [Gómez-Robles et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution, online 2023, volume 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-023-02253-z) compare 140 placental mammals. Their analyses attribute the unusual human neonatal/adult brain-size ratio primarily to postnatal enlargement and show that this ratio is not a sufficient measure of neurodevelopmental maturity. | Avoid treating a small proportion of adult brain volume as proof that a newborn is prematurely born or that most neural events moved outside the womb. Separate brain proportion, absolute size, growth, maturation and functional dependence. This result qualifies the explanation; it does not eliminate pelvic or maternal energetic constraints. |
| Extended development in early Homo, chapter 6 | [Zollikofer et al., Nature, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08205-2) analyse a Dmanisi juvenile with a mixed pattern of dental development, including slow early development and later rapid maturation. | Replace a simple ape-like/modern-human binary with a mosaic comparison. Some developmental changes may predate major brain enlargement; do not say that fully modern childhood was already established at Dmanisi. Dental evidence from an individual is not a complete measure of species-wide growth, social organisation or lifespan. |
| Neanderthal diet and new proxies, chapter 7 | [Jaouen et al., PNAS, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9618064/) apply enamel zinc isotopes to a Neanderthal individual and associated fauna at Gabasa, Spain, finding evidence of a high degree of carnivory. | Update the lecture's prospective discussion of zinc methods with an actual case. Retain the locality and individual as the scope. Compare proxies and local baselines; neither a carnivory signal nor plant residues determine an exact menu or percentage of all calories. This adds evidence, rather than simply invalidating the lecture's cautions. |
| Making fire, chapters 8 and 15 | [Sorensen et al., Scientific Reports, July 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-28342-9) infer fire-making from microwear on late Neanderthal tools. [Davis et al., Nature, online December 2025, volume 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09855-6) report a roughly 400,000-year-old Barnham context with heated sediments, fractured flint handaxes and pyrite fragments supporting deliberate fire-making. | Extend the timeline and distinguish fire occurrence, maintenance, ignition and obligatory reliance. These findings do not establish the first invention or universal dependence. Update the symposium's arguments about intermittent fire evidence in particular contexts without extrapolating them to all Neanderthal groups. |
| Smoke exposure and the Ah receptor, chapter 17 | [Aarts et al., Molecular Biology and Evolution, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8042735/) find similar responses of reconstructed Neanderthal and modern human Ah receptors to TCDD in human cells, contrary to earlier large differences measured in another cellular context. | Remove the smoke-receptor example as an established proof of a modern-human smoke-tolerance advantage. Use it optionally to teach how experimental context changes an evolutionary inference. The later experiment does not establish equivalence for every smoke compound or organismal detoxification pathway. |
| Dairying and lactase persistence, chapter 17 | [Evershed et al., Nature, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05010-7) combine archaeological and genetic evidence; prehistoric European milk use was widespread before high lactase-persistence frequencies. They propose famine and pathogen exposure as important selection contexts. | Preserve this example of gene–culture relationships while separating the adoption of dairying from allele-frequency change. Avoid a deterministic slider in which more milk immediately produces stronger selection. Famine and disease are proposed explanations supported by the authors' models, not a settled explanation for every region or lactase-persistence variant. |
| A fixed Dunbar number, chapter 18 | [Lindenfors et al., Biology Letters, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8103230/) reanalyse the comparative extrapolation and obtain highly uncertain, method-dependent estimates. | Remove a universal 150-person biological ceiling from both prose and network simulations. Present social-brain arguments and human network observations with their respective evidence. A critique of one numerical extrapolation does not refute all associations between cognition and social life. |

## Foundational distinctions to retain

Several corrections are about explanatory precision rather than the age of the source. [Navarrete et al., 2011](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10629) and [Pontzer et al., 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17654) already require a wider energetic framework than a simple brain–gut swap. Increasing throughput and reallocating expenditure are different mechanisms. The individual energy account must not enforce a constant budget across species.

The childhood figure should retain the empirical question in [Kuzawa et al., 2014](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1323099111). Clearly distinguish glucose uptake, inferred energetic demand, resting expenditure and total daily expenditure. A relationship between developmental curves is not direct proof that brain demand alone sets somatic growth in every context.

The cooking exercise should also include mechanical processing, grounded in [Zink and Lieberman, 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16990). More accessible food energy does not demonstrate when a technology arose. A measured present-day task cost is not automatically a calibrated cost for an extinct population.

## Questions requiring further review before authoring

- **Birth timing and obstetric constraints:** compare the original maternal energetic hypothesis with later empirical work. Keep pelvic dimensions, fetal growth, maternal metabolism and parturition mechanisms separate. No single study in this handoff establishes a universal ceiling or universal birth trigger.
- **Comparative brain organisation:** replace the literal reptilian/limbic/neocortical evolutionary stack with a current comparative account. The historical model may be introduced as history, but should not drive an interactive anatomical reconstruction. Selecting primary comparative studies for that replacement remains an authoring task.
- **Locomotion and endurance hunting:** separate demonstrated anatomical capacities, measured costs, archaeological evidence and hypotheses about selection. Check subsequent evidence before presenting persistence hunting as the necessary origin of the human body plan.
- **Thermoregulation and clothing:** update parasite divergence dates, anatomical reconstructions, brown-fat measurements and archaeological preservation claims. Do not infer that absence of a preserved needle proves absence of sewn clothing.
- **Ecological impacts and social organisation:** reassess region-specific extinction, landscape burning and early greenhouse-gas arguments. Keep multifactorial explanations and distinguish inference about cooperation from a prescribed ancestral division of labour.
- **Cortical genes and fossil blood supply:** distinguish experimental gene effects and anatomical proxies from demonstrated evolutionary causes. Audit each example against subsequent studies before choosing it as a core figure.

These are unresolved review tasks, not corrections already verified by this document. The source coverage matrix still determines which topics must be represented; later research changes the explanation and confidence attached to them.

## Evidence required before a chapter is published

Maintain a claim ledger with a stable claim ID, chapter, original lecture and exact caption interval, proposed teaching statement, primary source and publication date, relevant population or specimen, units, measured or inferred status, uncertainty, and classification as supported within scope, debated, superseded or unresolved. Record the reason for each revision and the date it was checked. A new finding can narrow a claim without replacing the whole conceptual model.

For numerical interactions, attach data origin, transformations, model assumptions and validation to every input series. An unresolved value must remain an explicit illustrative assumption or be omitted. New scientific findings should be part of the learner's explanation; expose detailed provenance through ordinary citations, with historical video links shown only when the learner enables the setting.
