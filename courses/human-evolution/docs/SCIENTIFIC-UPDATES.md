# Scientific revisions for the human evolution course

This targeted review records changes implemented in the interactive course’s historical source account. Hublin's lectures were delivered in 2017, following several studies published in 2016 or earlier; the symposium dates from 2018. Primary research available by 4 October 2026 supports the revisions below. The proposed teaching decisions are editorial interpretations of that evidence, not claims that every debate has been resolved.

These changes appear in the main course text; the ledger is in [Source coverage](SOURCE-COVERAGE.md). Where the optional YouTube references expose an older account, explain the difference and cite the newer paper. Automatic captions are useful for locating a claim but can misstate names, isotope labels and numbers. The transcript archive preserves them unchanged.

## Revisions supported by later primary research

| Topic and delivered chapter | Evidence to incorporate | Course decision and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menopause and post-reproductive survival, chapter 1 | [Wood et al., Science, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10645439/) report demographic and hormonal evidence of menopause in wild chimpanzees at Ngogo, Uganda. | Correct any categorical statement that wild chimpanzees do not experience substantial post-reproductive survival. Identify this as evidence from one community; it neither establishes a universal chimpanzee pattern nor by itself resolves the evolutionary explanation for human menopause. Keep grandmother and other hypotheses distinct from observations. |
| Neonatal development, chapter 2 | [Gómez-Robles et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution, online 2023, volume 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-023-02253-z) compare 140 placental mammals. Their analyses attribute the unusual human neonatal/adult brain-size ratio primarily to postnatal enlargement and show that this ratio is not a sufficient measure of neurodevelopmental maturity. | Avoid treating a small proportion of adult brain volume as proof that a newborn is prematurely born or that most neural events moved outside the womb. Separate brain proportion, absolute size, growth, maturation and functional dependence. This result qualifies the explanation; it does not eliminate pelvic or maternal energetic constraints. |
| Extended development in early Homo, chapter 2 | [Zollikofer et al., Nature, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08205-2) analyse a Dmanisi juvenile with a mixed pattern of dental development, including slow early development and later rapid maturation. | Replace a simple ape-like/modern-human binary with a mosaic comparison. Some developmental changes may predate major brain enlargement; do not say that fully modern childhood was already established at Dmanisi. Dental evidence from an individual is not a complete measure of species-wide growth, social organisation or lifespan. |
| Neanderthal diet and new proxies, chapter 3 | [Jaouen et al., PNAS, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9618064/) apply enamel zinc isotopes to a Neanderthal individual and associated fauna at Gabasa, Spain, finding evidence of a high degree of carnivory. | Update the lecture's prospective discussion of zinc methods with an actual case. Retain the locality and individual as the scope. Compare proxies and local baselines; neither a carnivory signal nor plant residues determine an exact menu or percentage of all calories. This adds evidence, rather than simply invalidating the lecture's cautions. |
| Making fire, chapters 3 and 5 | [Sorensen et al., Scientific Reports, July 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-28342-9) infer fire-making from microwear on late Neanderthal tools. [Davis et al., Nature, online December 2025, volume 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09855-6) report a roughly 400,000-year-old Barnham context with heated sediments, fractured flint handaxes and pyrite fragments supporting deliberate fire-making. | Extend the timeline and distinguish fire occurrence, maintenance, ignition and obligatory reliance. These findings do not establish the first invention or universal dependence. Update the symposium's arguments about intermittent fire evidence in particular contexts without extrapolating them to all Neanderthal groups. |
| Smoke exposure and the Ah receptor, chapter 6 | [Aarts et al., Molecular Biology and Evolution, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8042735/) find similar responses of reconstructed Neanderthal and modern human Ah receptors to TCDD in human cells, contrary to earlier large differences measured in another cellular context. | Remove the smoke-receptor example as an established proof of a modern-human smoke-tolerance advantage. Use it optionally to teach how experimental context changes an evolutionary inference. The later experiment does not establish equivalence for every smoke compound or organismal detoxification pathway. |
| Dairying and lactase persistence, chapter 6 | [Evershed et al., Nature, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05010-7) combine archaeological and genetic evidence; prehistoric European milk use was widespread before high lactase-persistence frequencies. They propose famine and pathogen exposure as important selection contexts. | Preserve this example of gene–culture relationships while separating the adoption of dairying from allele-frequency change. Avoid a deterministic slider in which more milk immediately produces stronger selection. Famine and disease are proposed explanations supported by the authors' models, not a settled explanation for every region or lactase-persistence variant. |
| A fixed Dunbar number, chapter 6 | [Lindenfors et al., Biology Letters, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8103230/) reanalyse the comparative extrapolation and obtain highly uncertain, method-dependent estimates. | Remove a universal 150-person biological ceiling from both prose and network simulations. Present social-brain arguments and human network observations with their respective evidence. A critique of one numerical extrapolation does not refute all associations between cognition and social life. |

## Foundational distinctions to retain

Several corrections are about explanatory precision rather than the age of the source. [Navarrete et al., 2011](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10629) and [Pontzer et al., 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17654) already require a wider energetic framework than a simple brain–gut swap. Increasing throughput and reallocating expenditure are different mechanisms. The individual energy account must not enforce a constant budget across species.

The childhood figure should retain the empirical question in [Kuzawa et al., 2014](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1323099111). Clearly distinguish glucose uptake, inferred energetic demand, resting expenditure and total daily expenditure. A relationship between developmental curves is not direct proof that brain demand alone sets somatic growth in every context.

The cooking exercise should also include mechanical processing, grounded in [Zink and Lieberman, 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16990). More accessible food energy does not demonstrate when a technology arose. A measured present-day task cost is not automatically a calibrated cost for an extinct population.

## Completed authoring audits

The handoff listed six further reviews. Each is now addressed in the delivered explanation, through a cited qualification or an explicit limit on inference. This completes the authoring decisions; it does not settle the underlying research questions.

| Audit | Implemented treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Birth timing and obstetric constraints | Chapter 2 compares Dunsworth et al. (2012) with [Haeusler et al.’s 2021 review](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12744). Pelvic constraints, fetal growth, maternal energy and birth mechanisms stay separate. No universal metabolic switch is simulated. |
| Comparative brain organisation | Chapter 6 replaces the literal reptilian/limbic/neocortical stack with homologous structures and changing cell/circuit organisation; [Tosches et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aar4237) supplies comparative single-cell evidence. |
| Locomotion and endurance hunting | Chapter 4 distinguishes measured comparative costs ([Sockol et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0703267104)), postcranial interpretation ([Daver et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04901-z)), and ethnographic occurrence/efficiency ([Morin and Winterhalder, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01876-x)). Feasibility is not an identified sole evolutionary cause. |
| Thermoregulation and clothing | Chapter 5 cites primate/clothing-louse studies, measured adult brown fat and modelled facial function, distinguishing parasite divergence, organismal physiology and fossil proxies. [Gilligan et al.’s 2024 review](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp2887) qualifies what eyed needles establish. No unpreserved garment is reconstructed as fact. |
| Ecological impacts and social organisation | Chapter 6 cites [Lemoine et al.’s 2023 global extinction analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ancene.2023.100403) and [Roebroeks et al.’s 2021 Neumark-Nord case](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj5567), with scale/locality limits. Early anthropogenic greenhouse-gas timing remains a hypothesis. Provisioning does not prescribe an ancestral division of labour. |
| Cortical genes and fossil blood supply | Chapter 6 treats [Heide et al.’s 2020 fetal marmoset experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abb2401) as a mechanism demonstration. Seymour et al.’s [2016 blood-flow model](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160305) is linked with its [2017 correction](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.170846); foramina do not directly measure fossil cognition. |

## Evidence and numerical figures

[Source coverage](SOURCE-COVERAGE.md) records stable claim IDs, chapter/lecture topic intervals, primary bibliography keys and dates, measured/inferred status, specimen or population scope, uncertainty and the editorial decision. Topic ranges are broad navigation aids checked against captions, not verified exact sentence boundaries. A historical video does not support a finding published later.

The numerical figures all use expressly declared illustrative inputs, rather than unresolved constants masquerading as measurements. Their units, transformations, assumptions and tests are recorded in the same document. The developmental curve is schematic; Kuzawa’s published peak equivalents are separately cited. The fossil guide and niche feedback are qualitative. No lecture slide, empirical plot or unlicensed fossil photograph was copied.

This is a targeted editorial review of central claims and identified source-era issues, not an exhaustive systematic literature review through 2026. The unchanged handoff ZIP preserves the original review and caption provenance.
