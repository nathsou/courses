# Human evolution interactive course plan

This proposal adapts all six lectures in Jean-Jacques Hublin's 2017 Collège de France series into an interactive textbook about energy, life history and human evolution. The central question is how our lineage could support costly brains, prolonged childhood, reproduction and long lives together. The reader should finish able to explain the trade-offs, trace the evidence behind those explanations, and recognise where several evolutionary accounts remain possible.

My recommendation is to make the six lectures the complete core and use selected 2018 symposium contributions within relevant chapters. Requiring the whole symposium would add repetition and specialist detail. Its strongest contributions are nevertheless valuable because they test and qualify the core explanation.

Status: handoff proposal, updated 4 October 2026. Curriculum, title, language and workload are recommendations, not agreed decisions. Optional YouTube source links and timestamps are a requested requirement. The course application has not been implemented. [Research provenance](../research/README.md), [caption manifest](../research/sources.json), [transcript archive](../research/hublin-transcripts.zip) and [scientific update review](SCIENTIFIC-UPDATES.md) accompany this plan.

The official lecture series took place in October–December 2017, and its associated symposium in June 2018. Some influential underlying studies date from 2016 or earlier. Preserve those dates while distinguishing the historical account from the updated course explanations.

## Scope and proposed reader

Working title: **Human Evolution**. Subtitle: **Energy, childhood, and the environments we make**. Proposed slug: `human-evolution`.

Write for a curious adult who has no training in palaeoanthropology or physiology. Teach evolutionary trees, geological time, comparative anatomy and basic energy accounting as needed. Ratios, graphs, simple algebra and probability are sufficient for the core; optional notes can explain allometry, isotope fractionation and demographic models. Coding should be an optional route for interested readers.

Use English to fit the collection, with original French lecture titles and a French–English glossary. An English adaptation should be new explanatory prose, preserving attribution and the structure of the source's arguments. Each chapter records relevant original lecture intervals; their YouTube links and timestamp labels are shown through the source-reference setting described below.

Proposed extent: a short opening chapter, 18 substantive chapters in six parts, and a synthesis chapter. Each chapter has two named sessions of roughly 15–25 minutes, with optional technical work clearly separated. Target 12–16 hours for the conceptual path, subject to piloting the first part. Video viewing is optional. The source lectures alone contain more than nine hours of material.

## Approaches considered

| Approach | What it does well | Main limitation | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A chronological journey through species | Gives fossils a memorable place in time | Encourages an apparent ladder from ape to human and fragments the physiological argument | Use a branching fossil atlas as a reference throughout |
| A simulation of building an energetically viable hominin | Connects diet, movement, growth and environment | An optimiser would encode speculative evolutionary causes as rules and make evolution appear purposeful | Use bounded models of specific processes, with assumptions visible |
| A sequence of fossil investigations | Makes inference and uncertainty central | Alone, it can lose the integrated explanation of energy and cooperation | Pair investigations with a recurring energy account; this is the main approach |

The resulting experience alternates between an explanation of a mechanism and an investigation of evidence for it. The reader follows energy from a landscape through an organism and into a household, then asks how teeth, skulls, footprints and archaeological remains constrain that account.

## Recurring tools

**An energy account** appears wherever it helps explain a chapter. It distinguishes energy acquired, metabolizable intake, expenditure, retained energy and transfers. Tabs separate the individual, household and landscape. Early chapters establish basal metabolism and total daily expenditure; later chapters add growth, foraging, thermoregulation and provisioning. A brain is part of basal expenditure, not an extra charge added to it. Heat and ATP expenditure are different views of the same processes and must not be counted twice.

This account is not a universally fixed pie. Readers compare reallocating energy with increasing overall throughput. A source-based comparative dataset can show the human–ape difference, while a labelled hypothetical exercise illustrates what a trade-off would mean. Choices in an illustrative model never create new fossil facts.

**An evidence notebook** holds the reader's predictions and short conclusions. Each investigation separates observation, inference and alternative explanations, then offers feedback on the strength of the conclusion. For example, a high nitrogen isotope value constrains diet but does not by itself reveal a complete menu. A useful final answer may be “the data do not distinguish these hypotheses.”

**A branching fossil atlas** situates specimens and studies in time and space. It distinguishes specimens, species and hypothesised relationships; uncertain relationships are shown as uncertain. Select a fossil to see what is preserved, what was reconstructed and which chapters use it. Modern apes are comparison species with their own evolutionary histories. Modern foraging societies are contemporary people, not living fossils.

These tools should stay compact within the text. A full workspace is optional. Avoid forcing readers to manage a dashboard before they understand the question.

### Lecture coverage notes

- **Lecture 1:** roughly 5–20 min establishes trophic ecology and energy acquisition; 20–40 min addresses metabolism and allocation; 40–68 min compares life histories; 68–80 min discusses provisioning, metabolic demand and fertility; 80–93 min addresses mortality, longevity and grandmother hypotheses. Keep survival curves and the timing of reproduction alongside the energy story. Introduce the historical r/K framing with qualifications rather than classifying every species into two boxes.
- **Lecture 2:** roughly 2–27 min covers comparative brain size and energetic accounts; 27–48 min addresses birth, maturation and social transfers; 48–68 min develops dental evidence and synchrotron methods; 68–102 min compares juvenile fossils and modern humans. This is the densest lecture. The fossil methods deserve a chapter of their own, with birth hypotheses explained in chapter 5 and revisited with fossil evidence in chapter 6.
- **Lecture 3:** roughly 5–36 min develops diet reconstruction and its limits; 36–61 min compares meat acquisition and returns; 61–74 min considers processing and fire; 74–94 min questions a simple hypercarnivore interpretation of Neanderthal diets. Its closing qualifications are central to the proposed isotope investigation.
- **Lecture 4:** roughly 3–31 min connects locomotor behaviour to anatomy; 31–49 min develops energetic comparisons and endurance hypotheses; 49–94 min walks through fossil and footprint evidence. Preserve the diversity and uncertainty of early bipeds; do not turn the lecture's comparative sequence into a single known ancestry.
- **Lecture 5:** roughly 2–29 min covers temperature control, evaporation, hair and parasite evidence; 29–43 min examines body proportions; 43–66 min relates physiological and anatomical variation to climate; 66–85 min treats housing and clothing. The archaeological clothing discussion is a substantive part of the course, not decorative background.
- **Lecture 6:** roughly 2–15 min synthesises energy and introduces niche construction; 15–43 min considers ecological impacts; 43–53 min introduces genetic feedback; 53–68 min addresses brain organisation and proposed genetic or vascular evidence; 68–91 min develops cooperation and social-brain hypotheses. Retain the social conclusion while auditing several particularly strong claims before teaching them.

## Interactions with a learning purpose

Build a small family of components with several uses. Each activity begins with a prediction, lets the reader inspect the consequence, and ends with an explanation of what was established. Most should take 5–10 minutes. A worked solution keeps later chapters accessible if the reader skips an exercise.

| Interaction | Reader action and discovery | Model or data boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trophic landscape | Move a hypothetical population between diets and resource landscapes; compare the area required to support it | Teach transfer and net production with labelled illustrative inputs. No universal 10% rule or uniquely determined prehistoric population density. |
| Life-history plots | Compare survival, maturation, reproductive timing and care across species; vary one toy schedule and see its consequences | Demographic models illustrate conditional outcomes. They do not prescribe a human social arrangement or derive fertility from calories alone. |
| Childhood timeline | Scrub age while viewing brain volume, glucose demand and body growth together; switch the denominator of the energy fraction | Use separately sourced curves. Resting metabolism and total expenditure have distinct labels. Volume is not an interchangeable proxy for metabolic cost. |
| Dental clock | Count incremental features in a schematic tooth, account for periodicity, and estimate an age interval | The first image is explicitly schematic. A real fossil exercise requires licensed imagery and a published reconstruction; uncertainty remains visible. |
| Diet investigation | Combine isotopes, tooth wear, calculus and fauna; vary the local isotope baseline and the tissue sampled | First use a synthetic case, then a cited dataset if available. Protein-source signals cannot be converted directly to the share of all dietary calories. |
| Processing and fire | Compare raw, sliced, pounded and cooked foods while including preparation, fuel gathering and group size | Separate chemical food energy, absorbed benefit, handling time and external fuel energy. Do not transplant mouse or novice-volunteer results into a universal hominin coefficient. |
| A kilometre of movement | Switch gait and speed, compare power with energy per distance, then add distance and load | Digitise or recover published curves before calibration. Separate measured living humans and apes from inferred fossil performance. |
| Heat balance | Change air temperature, humidity, exertion, body geometry and clothing; inspect pathways of heat exchange | Humidity constrains evaporation. Geometry models are teaching approximations and do not rank actual populations or predict individual physiology. |
| Evidence for clothing | Order genetic, archaeological and anatomical evidence by what it can establish and its dating uncertainty | Parasite divergence and surviving needles are indirect evidence with different inferential limits. Earliest preserved evidence is not necessarily first use. |
| Sharing through a difficult season | Compare the same mean resource return with different variation and transfer networks; inspect shortfalls and buffers | A seeded synthetic population makes assumptions inspectable. Demographic outcomes are conditional, with no claim that the model reproduces a named past society. |
| Niche feedback | Assemble a causal diagram for dairying, food use and genetic selection; test it against a chronology | Cultural adoption, genetic variation and changing allele frequencies are distinct events. The exercise compares accounts rather than rewarding one inevitable trajectory. |

The most promising first prototype is the childhood timeline paired with the sharing exercise. It reveals why brain volume alone misses the energetic peak, then connects prolonged dependence to transfers between people. Prototype the isotope investigation next: it tests whether the course can teach uncertainty as effectively as it teaches mechanisms.

### A sample session

Chapter 5 opens with a prediction about when the developing brain is most energetically demanding. The reader places a marker on an age axis before seeing the curves. The figure reveals separately sourced brain volume, glucose demand and growth rate; a toggle compares the fraction of resting expenditure with the fraction of total expenditure.

The reader then sees that being weaned does not imply being able to acquire enough food independently. An illustrative household has several people of different ages with visible acquisition and consumption assumptions. Food transfers can be rearranged, but calories cannot be created. Several arrangements can meet the same shortfall. Feedback asks the reader to explain what this supports about provisioning and what it cannot establish about a specific ancestral society.

The stopping point is a two-sentence conclusion saved in the notebook. The underlying study remains cited; enabling the video-reference setting also exposes the original lecture interval. The next session asks how much of this developmental account can actually be recovered from a juvenile fossil.

## Optional YouTube references in settings

Provide a course setting labelled **Show lecture video links and timestamps**, off by default. When enabled, relevant sections display the original lecture title, recording date, language, a human-readable time range and an **Open on YouTube at 27:00** link. Selected symposium references use the same setting and explicitly identify the speaker and symposium. The preference is remembered on this device and can be changed at any point without losing reading position, notebook drafts or exercise state.

References should be compact and adjacent to the passage they support. They open on YouTube only when clicked; no embedded player, autoplay or external-media request is needed on page load. Use ordinary accessible links that also work with keyboard and touch. The course remains fully readable with the setting off and without viewing a video. Bibliography entries, primary-paper citations, attribution and explanations of corrected claims remain visible independently of this setting.

Store structured source references with `videoId`, `startSeconds`, `endSeconds`, source title, speaker, language, recording date and a relationship to the course passage: background, supports, or historical claim revised. Generate URLs such as `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b5vIozqS40k&t=1620s`; display the end time as a label, since a normal YouTube link does not guarantee playback stops there. A historical reference must not imply that the recording supports a later correction. A revision callout links to the newer paper and, when enabled, the older video passage for comparison.

The broad intervals in the coverage notes are planning guides. Before publishing a reference, verify its precise start and end against the retrieved caption text, ensure the correct recording is selected and ensure the range lies within that recording's duration. No audio transcription is required. Acceptance checks cover the off/on preference after reload, references across chapters, timestamp URL generation, keyboard access and the absence of player requests when references are hidden.

## How to use the symposium

The associated material is formally a **colloquium**, not a parallel weekly seminar series. Its programme comprises 15 scientific presentations and opening and closing contributions. Fourteen scientific presentations are recorded; one is marked unrecorded. The caption review supports three levels of use. These are editorial recommendations, not rankings of scientific quality.

**Integrate selected findings into the core:** Pontzer for overall throughput and foraging costs; Kuzawa for the developmental brain-energy curve; Jaouen for isotope interpretation; Henry for the costs of fire; van Schaik for reliable energy supply and buffering. These talks refine central ideas already present in the lectures. They should supply a figure, a qualification or a short investigation, not become five additional mandatory video chapters.

**Offer optional investigations:** Gunz for reconstructed growth and birth hypotheses; McPherron for archaeological fire evidence; Speth and Rendu for dietary and seasonal alternatives. These make a strong optional sequence about what archaeologists can infer from an incomplete record.

**Keep the remaining talks as reference or extension material:** they add comparative reproduction, locomotor detail, Neanderthal demography, prey acquisition and microbiome mechanisms. In particular, Carmody adds a genuinely new biological scale, but making microbiome ecology a prerequisite would widen the core substantially.

## Scientific fidelity and updates

The course should preserve Hublin's questions and substantive coverage while teaching the strength of the evidence. Attribution does not require repeating a 2017 claim as present-day consensus. A caption is a source locator, not sufficient authority for a quantitative model.

Before writing chapters, record each substantive claim with the lecture interval, underlying primary publication, measured or inferred status, population, units, relevant uncertainty and later evidence. For every plotted series, record data origin, transformations and permissible reuse. Any synthetic teaching data must be labelled at the figure itself.

The [scientific update review](SCIENTIFIC-UPDATES.md) specifies concrete revisions from primary research published after the lectures, their limits and the chapters they affect. It includes the 2023 Ngogo chimpanzee findings, 2023/2024 comparative brain development, 2024 Dmanisi dental evidence, 2022 diet and lactase work, a 2021 smoke-receptor reassessment, and fire-making evidence published online in December 2025. Implement those revisions in the main explanation; a source-era video must not silently override them.

Several foundational issues also merit explicit treatment:

1. **Reallocation versus expanded throughput.** A single brain–gut exchange should not drive the course model. [Navarrete and colleagues' comparative study](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10629) challenges a simple brain–gut relationship; [Pontzer and colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17654) measure higher human expenditure after accounting for body size and activity. Compare mechanisms and empirical scope.
2. **The developmental brain peak.** [Kuzawa and colleagues](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1323099111) provide the central developmental study. Keep its measurement and normalization explicit. A fraction of resting expenditure must never become the same fraction of all daily expenditure in prose or code.
3. **Birth constraints.** Explain anatomical and maternal energetic hypotheses, including [Dunsworth and colleagues' study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3458333/), without coding either as a universal switch that fixes gestation. A current review of subsequent research is still needed.
4. **Processing before cooking.** [Zink and Lieberman's experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16990) makes slicing and pounding a useful mechanism to examine. Evidence for fire occurrence, habitual use and obligatory dependence addresses different questions. No control should assume that bigger Homo brains prove early cooking.
5. **Diet reconstruction.** Hublin and Jaouen explicitly discuss preservation, local baselines and competing explanations. Check later isotope results before fixing the case dataset. Keep the distinction between a diet dominated by animal protein and a diet composed entirely of meat.
6. **Niche construction and genetics.** Separate documented cultural practice from a proposed selective explanation. [Evershed and colleagues' 2022 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05010-7) is a priority for updating the lactase example. Audit the smoke-receptor account and cortical gene examples before committing to their causal interpretation.
7. **Social-brain claims.** Treat the social-brain hypothesis and language-as-grooming proposal as hypotheses with testable consequences. [A 2021 reanalysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8103230/) challenges a sharply specified Dunbar number. Avoid a hard 150-person cap in a network model. The lecture's layered reptilian/limbic/neocortical account also needs replacement with a current account of comparative brain organisation, retaining an explanatory note about the historical model if useful.
8. **Comparative life histories.** Recheck longevity, post-reproductive survival, fetal development, juvenile fossils and population estimates against newer primary research. Do not infer an ancestral average lifespan directly from a fossil mortality sample, or present a single sex division of labour as required by an energy account.

The update review is a targeted literature review, not a declaration that every source claim has been brought up to date. Each chapter must complete its claim ledger before publication. Modern medical, diet and exercise advice is outside the course's learning objective.

## Design and implementation direction

Use the visual language of a field notebook: restrained warm paper, clear ink, generous reading space and colour used consistently for quantities and evidence classes. Fossils and maps provide orientation; graphs and reconstructions provide the scientific work. A drawing of a reconstructed organism must be distinguished from a photograph or a directly preserved feature.

Reuse the collection's SvelteKit static-course pattern, Markdown directives, equation support and shared navigation. Inspect reusable components before copying them. Extend prediction questions, accessible plots, foldable details and saved notes. TypeScript modules can model energy accounting, basic demography, gait costs and heat exchange. SVG and Canvas 2D are enough initially; a 3D reconstruction is justified only if spatial inspection teaches something the simpler figure cannot.

Every interactive plot has a keyboard-operable control, readable axis definitions, a reset, a static summary or data table, and touch support. Motion respects reduced-motion preferences. Save notebook drafts and distinguish visited sections, self-reviewed explanations and automatically checked answers. Free-text scientific explanations should get a comparison rubric, not a claim of automated verification. All later activities have supplied reference examples, so unfinished earlier work never blocks the core.

Keep source captions and the research ZIP outside published assets. Publish original prose, verified attributed figures and source links. The archive is a repository handoff artifact. Do not add the course to the collection index until a coherent first learning path exists.

## Build sequence and acceptance criteria

1. **Establish the evidence base.** Apply the decisions in the scientific update review, complete the claim ledger, identify reusable datasets and licensed images, and resolve the remaining audits for the first chapters. Check exact intervals for any citations; verify every number against its publication. Classify each claim as supported within a stated scope, debated, superseded, or unresolved; unknown values must not become model constants.
2. **Pilot the two central learning modes.** Build the childhood/provisioning chapter and the diet-evidence investigation. Test whether a new reader can explain the denominator distinction, infer the need for transfers without overclaiming social structure, and identify an ambiguous isotope signal. Adjust scope and wording before expanding components.
3. **Write the complete core.** Add the 18 chapters in source order, with the fossil atlas, learning outcomes, natural stopping points, worked examples and cross-links. Track every substantive lecture topic in the coverage matrix, including the concluding social and ecological material.
4. **Add selected symposium depth.** Integrate core refinements first. Add optional investigations only when their prerequisites are already explained and they add a distinct reasoning task.
5. **Complete the synthesis and review.** The final case supplies clearly labelled measured, reconstructed and synthetic material. Require a coherent account, competing hypotheses and a request for discriminating evidence. Check model accounting, provenance, captions, optional video-reference settings, accessibility, mobile reading and saved-work behaviour, then integrate with the collection build and link audit.

The course is ready when a reader can follow the whole six-lecture argument, work with evidence rather than memorise an evolutionary story, and understand how bodily processes and collective support connect. The interaction quality matters more than the number of widgets. The first pilots should decide which of the proposed components earns its place.
