## Sections and letters

Phrases (Chapter 18) combine into **sections**, and sections into the **form** of a piece: its plan, the order in
which its material comes and comes back. Musicians label sections with letters. A new section gets the next
letter; a section that returns gets its old letter back; a prime marks a return with changes (A′). The forms in
this chapter are the shapes of dances, songs and short piano pieces, and most of the music you know is built
from them.

## Binary form

A **binary** piece has two sections, A and B, each usually repeated: ||: A :||: B :||. It is the form of most
Baroque dances. In a major key the A section often moves to the dominant; the B section starts away from home,
travels, and returns to the tonic for its final cadence. The two halves are often about the same length, and
the B section often begins with the A section’s opening idea, transposed.

The Minuet in G from the notebook Bach compiled for his wife Anna Magdalena (1725) is a binary piece; it is now
attributed to Christian Petzold. Its A section (bars 1–16) stays in :n[G] major, with an imperfect cadence at bar 8
and a perfect one at bar 16. Its B section (bars 17–32) moves to :n[D] major, with :n[C#] as its leading note, and
returns to :n[G].

## Rounded binary

When the B section ends by bringing back the opening of A, the form is **rounded binary**: ||: A :||: B A′ :||. The
return gives the piece a sense of homecoming, and it is the seed of sonata form (Chapter 29). The Ode to Joy
(Chapter 17) is a miniature of it: two phrases that open alike (a, a′), a contrasting phrase (b) that ends on the
dominant, and the second phrase again (a′).

```score caption="The Ode to Joy complete: a (bars 1–4), a′ (5–8), b (9–12), a′ (13–16). Rounded binary in sixteen bars." tempo="104"
\time 4/4 e''4^"a" e''4 f''4 g''4 | g''4 f''4 e''4 d''4 | c''4 c''4 d''4 e''4 | e''4. d''8 d''2 | e''4^"a′" e''4 f''4 g''4 | g''4 f''4 e''4 d''4 | c''4 c''4 d''4 e''4 | d''4. c''8 c''2 \bar "||" | d''4^"b" d''4 e''4 c''4 | d''4 e''8 f''8 e''4 c''4 | d''4 e''8 f''8 e''4 d''4 | c''4 d''4 g'2 | e''4^"a′" e''4 f''4 g''4 | g''4 f''4 e''4 d''4 | c''4 c''4 d''4 e''4 | d''4. c''8 c''2 |
```

## Ternary form

In **ternary** form, ABA, the first section is complete in itself (it ends in the tonic), the middle section
contrasts (another key, often the relative or parallel key or the dominant, and another character), and the first
section returns, sometimes exactly, sometimes decorated. Where binary is one argument in two halves, ternary is a
statement, a digression and a restatement.

Ternary form is everywhere: the **minuet and trio** of Classical symphonies and sonatas (a minuet, a contrasting
minuet called the trio, and the first minuet again, each of them a binary form in itself), the *da capo* aria of
Baroque opera, and countless Romantic piano pieces. Chopin’s “Raindrop” Prelude, Op. 28 No. 15, moves from
:n[Db] major to a dark middle section in :n[C#] minor and back.

## Song forms

Popular song has its own small forms, built from the same principles of repetition and contrast.

- **AABA** (the **32-bar song form**): four eight-:t[bar] sections, the third, B, a contrasting **bridge** (or
  “middle eight”). It is the form of most American standards of the 1920s–50s, the songbook jazz musicians still
  play.
- **Verse–chorus:** verses carry new words over the same music; the chorus repeats words and music and holds the
  hook. Many songs add a **pre-chorus** that builds towards the chorus, a **bridge** that contrasts once, late in
  the song, and an **outro**.
- **The 12-bar blues** (Chapter 15) is a form as well as a progression: the twelve :t[bar]s are one chorus, repeated
  as often as the song needs, and the lyrics often follow it as AAB, a line, the same line again, and an answer.

:::key
- Sections are labelled with letters; a returning section keeps its letter, a varied return gets a prime.
- **Binary:** ||: A :||: B :||, the A section often moving to the dominant, the B section returning to the tonic.
- **Rounded binary:** B ends with a return of A’s opening. **Ternary:** ABA with a contrasting, self-contained
  middle, as in the minuet and trio.
- Song forms: **AABA** (32 bars, with a bridge), **verse–chorus**, and the **12-bar blues**.
:::
