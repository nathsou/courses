This course is about theory, not technique, but theory at the piano needs a little technique, and this
appendix collects it. A teacher who can watch you play is worth more than any page; what follows is the
minimum that every method agrees on.

## Sitting and the hand

- Sit at the front half of the bench, opposite middle C, with your forearms roughly level with the keys and
  your elbows a little in front of your body. Your feet should rest flat (or on the pedals).
- Curve your fingers as if holding a ball; play with the fingertips, the thumb on its outer corner.
- Let the arm’s weight do the work. Pressing harder than the key needs makes tension, and tension makes
  wrong notes.
- Fingers are numbered from the thumb in both hands: **1** thumb, **2** index, **3** middle, **4** ring,
  **5** little finger.

## Fingering principles

1. **The thumb passes under, and fingers cross over**, so that a scale or a long line never runs out of fingers.
2. **The thumb avoids black keys** in scales and arpeggios: it is short and sits low.
3. **The same black key gets the same finger** in every octave of a scale, which is what makes a fingering
   learnable.
4. **Fingering serves the music**: choose the fingering that keeps a line smooth and lets the hand stay in
   one position for as long as possible. Chords and voicings (Chapter 11) are fingered by shape: the hand
   moves to the next chord as a unit, with shared notes held by the same finger where possible.

## Major scales

One octave, going up; going down, the same fingers in reverse. “|” marks where the thumb passes under (or
a finger crosses over).

| Scale | Right hand | Left hand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| :n[C], :n[G], :n[D], :n[A], :n[E] | 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 5 | 5 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 |
| :n[B] | 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 5 | 4 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 |
| :n[F] | 1 2 3 4 \| 1 2 3 4 | 5 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 |
| :n[F#] / :n[Gb] | 2 3 4 \| 1 2 3 \| 1 2 | 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 \| 4 |
| :n[Db] / :n[C#] | 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 \| 1 2 | 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 \| 3 |
| :n[Ab] | 3 4 \| 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 | 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 \| 3 |
| :n[Eb] | 3 \| 1 2 3 4 \| 1 2 3 | 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 \| 3 |
| :n[Bb] | 4 \| 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 | 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 \| 3 |

For the black-key scales, the pattern is easiest to remember from the black keys themselves: in the right
hand, finger **4 always plays :n[Bb]** (in :n[F] major too); the thumbs play only white keys.

## Minor scales (harmonic)

Minor scales that start on a white key use the fingering of their parallel major (only the notes change):

| Scale | Right hand | Left hand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| :n[A], :n[E], :n[D], :n[G], :n[C] minor | 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 5 | 5 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 |
| :n[B] minor | 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 4 5 | 4 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 |
| :n[F] minor | 1 2 3 4 \| 1 2 3 4 | 5 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 |
| :n[F#] minor | 3 4 \| 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 | 4 3 2 1 \| 3 2 1 \| 4 |
| :n[C#] minor | 3 4 \| 1 2 3 \| 1 2 3 | 3 2 1 \| 4 3 2 1 \| 3 |

For :n[G#], :n[Eb] and :n[Bb] minor, published fingerings differ from one edition to another; follow your
scale book or teacher, and apply the principles above (thumbs on white keys, the same finger on the same
black key).

## The sustain pedal

The right-hand pedal lifts the dampers off the strings, so notes keep sounding after you let go of the
keys. MIDI keyboards send it as *control change 64* (the course listens for it).

- **Harmonic pedalling.** Change the pedal when the harmony changes: hold it through one chord, and release
  and press it again as the next chord sounds, so the two do not blur.
- **Legato pedalling** (or *syncopated* pedalling): play the new chord, *then* lift and re-press the pedal
  straight after it. The pedal catches the new chord and lets go of the old one, so the sound never breaks.
  It is the pedalling most piano music needs.
- Melodies that move by step usually need the pedal changed on every note, or no pedal at all: two
  neighbouring notes held together are a dissonance (Chapter 8), not a melody.

## Practising

Short, daily, slow. Ten minutes of scales and chords in a new key each day (Chapter 7’s circle is a good
order) does more than an hour once a week. Practise hands separately before together, slowly enough to
play everything right, and only then faster. The [practice room](/practice/) schedules the theory side of
that routine for you.
