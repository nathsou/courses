The course notates and plays only three kinds of music: music in the **public domain**, music **written for the
course**, and **chord progressions**, which are not protected by copyright. Songs that are still in copyright are
named, and their progressions discussed, but never notated or played.

Every score in the course is engraved by the course itself, from its own text format, and every sound is played by
its own piano. Where notes come from an edition, the edition is public domain and is named below; where a modern
engraving was used to read or check the notes, it is credited too.

## Written for the course

Every other score, exercise and backing track was written for the course: the examples of rhythm, metre and
notation; the phrases and progressions in four voices; the cantus firmus and the counterpoints of Chapter 20; the
figured basses and melodies of the realisation exercises; the lead-sheet tunes and improvisation charts of Part VI;
the planing melody of Chapter 38; and the twelve-tone row of Chapter 41.

## Named, not notated

These pieces are discussed for their harmony or form, or suggested for listening, but no note of them is notated,
quoted or played. Where a progression is played (the two chords of Satie’s first *Gymnopédie*, the Petrushka chord),
it is only the chords, which are not protected.

- **Classical and modern:** Beethoven’s *Für Elise*; Mozart’s Variations on “Ah, vous dirai-je, maman”, K. 265;
  Debussy’s Préludes (“La cathédrale engloutie”, “La fille aux cheveux de lin”, “Voiles”); Satie’s *Gymnopédies*;
  Stravinsky’s *Petrushka*; Schoenberg’s Three Piano Pieces Op. 11, Suite Op. 25 and Piano Piece Op. 33a.
- **Songs:** the songs used to recognise intervals in Chapter 8; *Frère Jacques* and *Row, Row, Row Your Boat*;
  *Scarborough Fair*; the pop songs that share the four chords of the prologue; “Creep”; “Oye Como Va”.
- **Jazz:** “Autumn Leaves”, “So What”, “Giant Steps” and Gershwin’s “I Got Rhythm” (the source of rhythm changes).

Chapter 41 uses a twelve-tone row written for the course rather than Schoenberg’s, because the course could not check
Op. 33a’s row against a printed edition.
