---
title: 'Waiting for, thinking of'
goals:
  - use the commonest verbs, adjectives and nouns with fixed prepositions, in the right case
  - ask about things with worauf, woran, wofür and about people with auf wen, an wen, mit wem
  - refer back to a thing with darauf, daran, damit, and to a person with auf ihn, an sie
  - point ahead to a whole clause, as in Ich freue mich darauf, dich zu sehen
---

English waits *for* a bus, thinks *of* home and is interested *in* jazz. German has the same habit of tying prepositions to verbs, but the prepositions almost never line up: German waits *on* the bus ({warten auf}), thinks *at* home ({denken an}) and is interested *for* jazz ({sich interessieren für}). There is no logic you can work out from English. You learn verb, preposition and case together, as one unit, just as you learnt {der Tisch} rather than "Tisch".

The reward is large. About thirty of these combinations cover most of everyday German, and once they are in place, two tidy word families, {darauf, daran, damit} and {worauf, woran, womit}, make you sound fluent rather than translated. If you studied German before, you may remember the list but not the case, or you may hear your English brain saying "{Ich warte für den Bus}". This lesson fixes both.

## Warten auf, denken an

```dialogue
title: An der Bushaltestelle
Jonas: Worauf wartest du denn? Der Bus ist doch gerade weg. | What are you waiting for? The bus has just gone.
Zeynep: Ich warte nicht auf den Bus. Ich warte auf Mia. | I'm not waiting for the bus. I'm waiting for Mia.
Jonas: Ach so. Und woran denkst du? Du siehst so ernst aus. | I see. And what are you thinking about? You look so serious.
Zeynep: An meine Prüfung morgen. Ich habe richtig Angst davor. | About my exam tomorrow. I'm really scared of it.
Jonas: Quatsch, du bist super vorbereitet. Denk lieber an das Wochenende! | Nonsense, you're really well prepared. Think about the weekend instead!
Zeynep: Stimmt. Ich freue mich schon auf die Party bei Aylin. | True. I'm already looking forward to the party at Aylin's.
Jonas: Siehst du? Und es hängt ja nicht nur von der Prüfung ab, ob du einen guten Abschluss machst. | See? And it doesn't only depend on the exam whether you get a good degree.
```

Each of these verbs has its own preposition, and the preposition is part of the meaning. {Ich denke an dich} is "I'm thinking of you"; change the preposition and you change the verb: {Ich denke über deinen Vorschlag nach} is "I'm thinking your suggestion over".

```words
warten (auf + A) | to wait (for)
denken (denkt, dachte, hat gedacht) (an + A) | to think (of)
nach|denken (denkt nach, dachte nach, hat nachgedacht) (über + A) | to think about, to consider
sich freuen (auf + A) (über + A) | to look forward to (auf); to be pleased about (über)
sich interessieren (für + A) | to be interested (in)
sich erinnern (an + A) | to remember
sich kümmern (um + A) | to look after, to take care of
träumen (von + D) | to dream (of)
teil|nehmen (nimmt teil, nahm teil, hat teilgenommen) (an + D) | to take part (in)
ab|hängen (hängt ab, hing ab, hat abgehangen) (von + D) | to depend (on)
```

### Which case?

Prepositions that always take one case keep it here too: **{für}** and **{um}** take the accusative, **{mit}, {von}, {bei}, {nach}, {zu}, {aus}** the dative. The tricky ones are the two-way prepositions ({an}, {auf}, {in}, {über}, {vor}). After a verb like {warten} they no longer describe a place or a direction, so the wo?/wohin? test you learnt at A2 does not work. The case is simply fixed for each verb. Two rules of thumb get you most of the way:

| Preposition | Usual case after a verb | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {auf}, {über} | accusative, almost always | {warten auf den Bus}, {sich freuen über das Geschenk} |
| {vor} | dative | {Angst haben vor dem Hund} |
| {an} | it depends: learn each one | {denken an den Urlaub} (A), but {teilnehmen an dem Kurs} (D) |

:::mistake
Three classic English-speaker slips. {Ich warte für dich} means "I'm waiting on your behalf"; "I'm waiting for you" is {Ich warte auf dich}. {Ich denke über dich} is not German at all; "I'm thinking of you" is {Ich denke an dich}. And "I'm interested in music" is {Ich interessiere mich für Musik}, never {in Musik}.
:::

:::tip
Learn each combination inside a short sentence with an article, so the case comes for free: not "warten auf + A" but {Ich warte auf den Bus}. Say it aloud a few times. Your ear will soon tell you that {auf dem Bus} sounds wrong.
:::

## Sich freuen auf, über, für

{sich freuen} takes three prepositions, and each one tells you something different:

- **{auf}** + A: something still to come. {Ich freue mich auf den Urlaub.} I'm looking forward to the holiday.
- **{über}** + A: something that has happened or is here now. {Ich freue mich über dein Geschenk.} I'm pleased with your present.
- **{für}** + A: someone else's good luck. {Ich freue mich für dich!} I'm happy for you!

{über} is the preposition of reactions in general: you are annoyed about, complain about, laugh about and get worked up about things {über} + accusative. Thanking and apologising take two prepositions, {bei} for the person and {für} for the reason, usually in that order: {Ich möchte mich bei Ihnen für die Hilfe bedanken.}

```words
sich ärgern (über + A) | to be annoyed (about)
sich auf|regen (über + A) | to get worked up (about)
sich beschweren (über + A) (bei + D) | to complain (about; to)
sich bedanken (bei + D) (für + A) | to thank (someone for something)
sich entschuldigen (bei + D) (für + A) | to apologise (to someone for something)
```

## More verbs, adjectives and nouns

A handful more verbs you will need every week:

```words
sich gewöhnen (an + A) | to get used to
sich verlassen (verlässt, verließ, hat verlassen) (auf + A) | to rely (on)
bitten (bittet, bat, hat gebeten) (um + A) | to ask (for)
sich vor|bereiten (auf + A) | to prepare (for)
sich beschäftigen (mit + D) | to deal with, to be busy with
```

- {Ich kann mich nicht an das frühe Aufstehen gewöhnen.} I can't get used to getting up early.
- {Auf Hannah kann man sich immer verlassen.} You can always rely on Hannah.
- {Darf ich Sie um einen Gefallen bitten?} May I ask you a favour?
- {Ben bereitet sich auf das Vorstellungsgespräch vor.} Ben is preparing for the interview.
- {In meiner Masterarbeit beschäftige ich mich mit sozialen Medien.} My master's thesis deals with social media.

:::mistake
{bitten um} (to ask *for* something) is not {fragen} (to ask a question). {Er bittet um Hilfe} is "he asks for help"; {Er fragt nach dem Weg} is "he asks the way". And {sich erinnern an} means "to remember"; without {sich} it means "to remind": {Erinnere mich bitte an den Termin!} Remind me about the appointment, please!
:::

Adjectives and nouns carry prepositions too. Nouns usually keep the preposition of their verb ({sich erinnern an} → {die Erinnerung an}), but not always: {sich interessieren für}, yet {das Interesse an}.

```words
stolz (auf + A) | proud (of)
zufrieden (mit + D) | satisfied, happy (with)
gespannt (auf + A) | curious (about), eager to see
begeistert (von + D) | enthusiastic (about), thrilled (with)
verliebt (in + A) | in love (with)
die Angst, ¨-e (vor + D) | fear (of)
das Interesse, -n (an + D) | interest (in)
die Erinnerung, -en (an + A) | memory (of)
die Hoffnung, -en (auf + A) | hope (of, for)
```

## Worauf? Auf wen?

To ask about a **thing**, German glues {wo} onto the preposition: {Worauf wartest du?} What are you waiting for? If the preposition begins with a vowel, an **r** slips in so the two vowels do not collide: {wo + auf} → {worauf}, {wo + an} → {woran}, {wo + über} → {worüber}, {wo + um} → {worum}.

| Preposition | Question about a thing | Question about a person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {auf} | {Worauf wartest du?} | {Auf wen wartest du?} |
| {an} | {Woran denkst du?} | {An wen denkst du?} |
| {über} | {Worüber ärgerst du dich?} | {Über wen ärgerst du dich?} |
| {für} | {Wofür interessierst du dich?} | {Für wen ist das Geschenk?} |
| {mit} | {Womit beschäftigst du dich?} | {Mit wem sprichst du?} |
| {von} | {Wovon träumst du?} | {Von wem träumst du?} |

To ask about a **person**, keep the preposition and add {wen} (accusative) or {wem} (dative), whichever the preposition needs. {wo-} words are only for things and ideas.

:::note
In everyday speech, especially in the south, you will often hear {Auf was wartest du?} or {Mit was …?} instead of {worauf} and {womit}. It is colloquial: fine among friends, but write {worauf}. And one question you will hear constantly is {Worum geht es?}, "What's it about?", from {es geht um} + accusative: {Worum geht es in dem Film?} – {Es geht um eine Familie in Graz.}
:::

```words
worauf | what … for, on what (wo + auf)
woran | what … of, at what (wo + an)
```

## Darauf, daran: pointing back

When you answer, you do not want to repeat the noun. For a **thing**, use {da-} + preposition (with {dar-} before a vowel): {darauf}, {daran}, {darüber}, {darum}, but {dafür}, {damit}, {davon}, {davor}. For a **person**, keep the preposition and use a pronoun.

- {Wartest du auf den Bus?} – {Ja, ich warte darauf.} (a thing)
- {Wartest du auf Jonas?} – {Ja, ich warte auf ihn.} (a person)
- {Hast du Angst vor der Prüfung?} – {Nein, ich habe keine Angst davor.}
- {Hast du Angst vor dem Hund?} – {Ein bisschen. Ich habe vor allen Hunden Angst.}

:::grammar[Why not "auf es"?]
English says "I'm waiting for it", so learners build {auf es} or {an ihn} for things. German does not like a preposition followed by a pronoun that means a thing: it uses the {da-} word instead. {Ich denke an es} is simply wrong; {Ich denke daran} is right. Spoken German shortens these words: {drauf}, {dran}, {drüber}. In the north you will even hear them split: {Da hab ich keine Lust drauf.} That is colloquial; write {Darauf habe ich keine Lust.}
:::

```words
darauf | on it, for it (da + auf)
daran | of it, about it (da + an)
```

## Ich freue mich darauf, dass …

Here is where the {da-} words really earn their keep. English can put a whole idea after a preposition: "I'm looking forward to *seeing you*", "it depends on *whether it rains*". German cannot put a clause straight after {auf} or {von}, so the {da-} word stands in the main clause as a placeholder, and the clause follows after a comma:

- {Ich freue mich darauf, dich zu sehen.} (a zu-infinitive: same subject)
- {Ich freue mich darauf, dass du kommst.} (a dass-clause: different subject)
- {Es hängt davon ab, ob es regnet.} (an ob-clause)
- {Erinnerst du dich daran, wie wir uns kennengelernt haben?} (a w-clause)

With some verbs the placeholder is optional: {Ich erinnere mich (daran), dass …}, {Ich habe Angst (davor), allein zu reisen.} With others, such as {warten}, {abhängen} and {sich verlassen}, it is needed: {Wir warten darauf, dass der Regen aufhört.} When in doubt, put it in; it is never wrong.

:::key
One of the most useful phrases in German comes from {ankommen auf} + A: **{Es kommt darauf an.}** It depends. Add a clause to say on what: {Es kommt darauf an, wie viel Zeit wir haben.}
:::

```words
Es kommt darauf an. | It depends.
```
