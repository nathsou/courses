Here is the same piece of news three times:

- {Weil es stark geregnet hat, haben sie das Konzert abgesagt.} (a friend on the phone)
- {Wegen des starken Regens wurde das Konzert abgesagt.} (the newspaper)
- {Konzertabsage wegen Starkregen} (the headline)

As German gets more formal, the information moves out of verbs and into **nouns**. This is the **Nominalstil**, the house style of newspapers, official letters, contracts and academic writing. In its worst form it gives you sentences like {Die Inanspruchnahme der Leistung erfolgt nach vorheriger Antragstellung}, which even Germans have to read twice. Used well, it is compact and precise.

At B2 you need both directions: to *read* nominal German without getting lost, and to *write* a little of it in a formal email or an essay, without overdoing it. This lesson shows how the conversion works, gives you the fixed noun-verb pairs that formal German loves, and ends with the German compound noun, the nominal style's favourite tool.

## Das Lesen, die Entwicklung

German makes nouns from verbs in a handful of ways:

| type | examples | gender |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **the infinitive** | {lesen} → {das Lesen}, {Rad fahren} → {das Radfahren} | always {das} |
| **-ung** | {entwickeln} → {die Entwicklung}, {verbessern} → {die Verbesserung} | always {die} |
| **the bare stem**, often with a vowel change | {beginnen} → {der Beginn}, {beschließen} → {der Beschluss}, {ansteigen} → {der Anstieg}, {umziehen} → {der Umzug} | mostly {der} |
| **-t** | {ankommen} → {die Ankunft}, {abfahren} → {die Abfahrt} | {die} |
| **-e** | {suchen} → {die Suche}, {helfen} → {die Hilfe} | {die} |

And from adjectives with **-heit**, **-keit** and **-igkeit** (always {die}): {sicher} → {die Sicherheit}, {möglich} → {die Möglichkeit}, {arbeitslos} → {die Arbeitslosigkeit}.

The infinitive noun is the easiest of all, and very common with prepositions: {beim Kochen} (while cooking), {zum Lernen} (for learning), {vom vielen Sitzen} (from all that sitting).

:::key
When a verb becomes a noun, **the rest of the sentence changes too**:

- the subject or object becomes a **genitive** (or {von} + dative): {Der Zug kommt an.} → {die Ankunft des Zuges}; {Man entwickelt einen Impfstoff.} → {die Entwicklung eines Impfstoffs}
- an adverb becomes an **adjective**: {Die Preise steigen stark.} → {der starke Anstieg der Preise}
- a prepositional object usually keeps its preposition: {Man fragt nach Wohnungen.} → {die Nachfrage nach Wohnungen}
:::

```words
die Entwicklung, -en | development
die Verbesserung, -en | improvement
die Erhöhung, -en | increase, rise
der Anstieg, -e | rise, increase
der Rückgang, ¨-e | fall, decline
der Verlust, -e | loss
der Beschluss, ¨-e | decision, resolution
die Nachfrage, -n | demand; enquiry
die Voraussetzung, -en | precondition, requirement
die Arbeitslosigkeit (Sg.) | unemployment
erfolgen (erfolgt, erfolgte, ist erfolgt) | to take place, to be carried out (formal)
```

## Weil es regnete → wegen des Regens

Most conjunctions that start a subordinate clause have a preposition that does the same job with a noun. This table is the core of the nominal style:

| verbal style (clause) | nominal style (preposition + noun) |
| --- | --- |
| {weil es stark regnete} | {wegen des starken Regens} / {aufgrund des starken Regens} |
| {obwohl es kalt war} | {trotz der Kälte} |
| {nachdem er angekommen war} | {nach seiner Ankunft} |
| {bevor die Sitzung beginnt} | {vor Beginn der Sitzung} |
| {während sie studierte} | {während ihres Studiums} |
| {seit sie umgezogen ist} | {seit ihrem Umzug} |
| {wenn es regnet} / {falls es Probleme gibt} | {bei Regen} / {bei Problemen} |
| {um Energie zu sparen} | {zum Energiesparen} / {zur Einsparung von Energie} |
| {damit die Sicherheit verbessert wird} | {zur Verbesserung der Sicherheit} |
| {indem man die Preise senkt} | {durch die Senkung der Preise} |

Remember the cases: {wegen}, {trotz}, {während} and {aufgrund} take the genitive (B1); {nach}, {vor}, {seit}, {bei}, {zu} the dative; {durch} the accusative.

::nominal-style

## Too much of a good thing

Nominal style belongs where information must be dense: headlines, reports, official notices, academic texts. In speech it sounds stiff, and even in writing too much of it becomes the dreaded **Amtsdeutsch** (officialese), which every German loves to mock.

| Amtsdeutsch | plain German |
| --- | --- |
| {Die Inanspruchnahme des Bustransfers erfolgt nach vorheriger Anmeldung.} | {Wenn Sie den Bus nutzen wollen, müssen Sie sich vorher anmelden.} |
| {Bei Nichterscheinen erfolgt keine Rückerstattung der Gebühr.} | {Wenn Sie nicht kommen, bekommen Sie die Gebühr nicht zurück.} |
| {Nach Eingang Ihrer Zahlung erfolgt der Versand der Ware.} | {Sobald Sie bezahlt haben, schicken wir Ihnen die Ware.} |

Notice the verb that does no work at all: **{erfolgen}** ("to take place"). It is the signature of Amtsdeutsch. When you see it, the real action is in the noun next to it.

:::tip
For your own writing: in a formal email or an essay, a few nominal phrases ({aufgrund der hohen Nachfrage}, {nach Rücksprache mit meiner Chefin}) make you sound precise. Two in one sentence is usually the limit. If you need to read a sentence twice, rewrite it with verbs.
:::

## Eine Entscheidung treffen: Funktionsverbgefüge

Formal German is full of fixed pairs in which a noun carries the meaning and a verb does little more than hold the sentence together: {eine Entscheidung treffen} instead of {entscheiden}, {Kritik üben} instead of {kritisieren}. Grammarians call them **Funktionsverbgefüge**; English has a few too ("make a decision", "take into account").

Why use them? They sound more formal and weighty; some say things the simple verb cannot ({zur Verfügung stehen} has no one-word equivalent); and some have a passive or a "beginning" meaning ({zur Diskussion stehen} = is being discussed; {in Gang kommen} = get going).

The catch: the verb is not predictable. It is {eine Entscheidung treffen}, never *{eine Entscheidung machen}; {eine Frage stellen}, never *{eine Frage fragen}. Learn each pair as one unit.

| Funktionsverbgefüge | plain verb | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {eine Entscheidung treffen} | {entscheiden} | to make a decision |
| {zur Verfügung stellen} | {bereitstellen} | to provide, make available |
| {zur Verfügung stehen} | {verfügbar sein} | to be available |
| {infrage kommen} | {möglich sein} | to be an option |
| {infrage stellen} | {bezweifeln} | to call into question |
| {Kritik üben} (an + D) | {kritisieren} | to criticise |
| {Bescheid geben / sagen} | {informieren} | to let someone know |
| {zum Ausdruck bringen} | {ausdrücken} | to express |
| {in Kraft treten} | {gültig werden} | to come into force |
| {eine Rolle spielen} | {wichtig sein} | to matter |
| {Rücksicht nehmen} (auf + A) | {berücksichtigen} | to show consideration (for) |
| {in Betracht ziehen} | {erwägen} | to consider |
| {zur Sprache bringen} | {ansprechen} | to raise (a subject) |
| {Maßnahmen ergreifen} | {handeln} | to take measures |

:::note
{infrage} is the spelling the Duden recommends; {in Frage} is also correct and you will see both.
:::

## Wohnungsmarkt, Straßenbahn: compound nouns

The nominal style's favourite tool is the compound noun. Instead of {die Erhöhung der Miete}, a headline writes {die Mieterhöhung}; instead of {der Markt für Wohnungen}, {der Wohnungsmarkt}.

Two rules from A1 still hold: the **last part** decides the gender and the basic meaning ({die Wohnung} + {der Markt} = **der** {Wohnungsmarkt}, a kind of market), and the parts before it narrow it down. What makes compounds tricky is the **linking element** (Fugenelement) between the parts:

| link | when | examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-s-** | always after -ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, -tät, -schaft; often after other words too | {Wohnungsmarkt}, {Sicherheitsrisiko}, {Universitätsstadt}, {Arbeitsplatz} |
| **-(e)n-** | after many feminine nouns in -e, and n-nouns | {Straßenbahn}, {Sonnenbrille}, {Studentenwohnheim} |
| **-er-**, **-e-** | often a plural form | {Kinderzimmer}, {Hundeleine} |
| nothing, or -e dropped | many short words; verbs lose their ending | {Hausschlüssel}, {Sprachkurs}, {Schulbuch}, {Schreibtisch} |

:::fun
German compounds have no length limit. The record holder for years was the {Rindfleischetikettierungsüberwachungsaufgabenübertragungsgesetz} (the law delegating the monitoring of beef labelling), passed in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania in 1999 and repealed in 2013, to the regret of linguists everywhere.
