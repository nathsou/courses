{In Deutschland wird viel recycelt.} {Das Brot wird jeden Morgen frisch gebacken.} {Hier wird nicht geraucht.}

Who recycles? Who bakes? Who doesn't smoke? It doesn't matter, and that is the point of the **passive**: it puts the action in the spotlight and leaves the doer in the dark. German uses it a great deal in instructions, notices, recipes, news and history, and it builds it differently from English. English says "is baked"; German says "**becomes** baked": {wird gebacken}. If you remember one thing from this lesson, make it that.

The theme is how things get done: how bread is made in a Salzburg bakery, how a Graz coffee house came back to life, and why German kitchens have four bins.

## werden + participle

{Die Brötchen werden um vier Uhr gebacken.} The rolls are baked at four o'clock.

The passive is the verb {werden} plus the participle at the end of the sentence. Only {werden} changes:

| | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| {ich werde gefragt} | I am asked | {wir werden gefragt} | we are asked |
| {du wirst gefragt} | you are asked | {ihr werdet gefragt} | you (all) are asked |
| {er/sie/es wird gefragt} | he/she/it is asked | {sie/Sie werden gefragt} | they/you are asked |

To turn an active sentence into a passive one, the **accusative object becomes the subject**, so it moves into the nominative:

- active: {Man repariert den Wagen.} Someone is repairing the car.
- passive: {Der Wagen wird repariert.} The car is being repaired.

{den Wagen} (accusative) has become {der Wagen} (nominative).

The person doing it disappears, or comes back with {von} (later in this lesson): {Das Brot wird vom Bäcker gebacken.}

:::mistake
English "is done" is **not** German {ist gemacht}. {Die Fenster werden jede Woche geputzt} means "the windows are cleaned every week". {Die Fenster sind geputzt} means "the windows are clean", a finished state (that is the state passive, a B2 topic). If an action is happening or happens regularly, you need {werden}.
:::

{werden} now has three jobs, and you can always tell them apart by what comes with it:

| werden + | meaning | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a noun or adjective | become | {Sie wird Ärztin.} {Es wird dunkel.} |
| an infinitive | future | {Sie wird morgen anrufen.} |
| a participle | passive | {Sie wird oft angerufen.} |

```words

reparieren | to repair
die Reparatur, -en | repair
erlauben | to allow
verbieten (verbietet, verbot, hat verboten) | to forbid, to ban
```

## How bread is made

Processes are the passive's home ground. Nobody cares which baker kneads the dough at two in the morning; what matters is what happens to the dough.

```words

das Mehl (Sg.) | flour
die Zutat, -en | ingredient
die Bäckerei, -en | bakery
der Ofen, ¨ | oven
kneten | to knead
mischen | to mix
formen | to shape
backen (backt, backte, hat gebacken) | to bake
schneiden (schneidet, schnitt, hat geschnitten) | to cut
```

:::note
{backen} has two present forms: {er backt} and the older {er bäckt}. Both are correct. In the Präteritum, {backte} has replaced the old {buk} almost everywhere.
:::

## The past: wurde … and ist … worden

- {Das Kaffeehaus wurde 1894 gegründet.} The coffee house was founded in 1894.
- {Es ist im letzten Jahr renoviert worden.} It was renovated last year.

The passive has the same tenses as any other verb. Only {werden} changes; the participle stays at the end:

| Tense | Passive | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Präsens | {Das Haus wird gebaut.} | now, regularly |
| Präteritum | {Das Haus wurde gebaut.} | written past, narration, news |
| Perfekt | {Das Haus ist gebaut worden.} | spoken past; the opening line of news items |
| Plusquamperfekt | {Das Haus war gebaut worden.} | before another past event |

Two things to watch in the Perfekt:

- The auxiliary is always **{sein}**, because {werden} takes {sein}: {ist … worden}, {sind … worden}.
- The participle of passive {werden} is **{worden}**, without ge-. {geworden} is for "become": {Sie ist Ärztin geworden}, but {Sie ist operiert worden}.

::passive-machine

```words
eröffnen | to open (officially)
renovieren | to renovate
entdecken | to discover
zerstören | to destroy
das Gebäude, - | building
das Kaffeehaus, ¨-er | coffee house (Austrian)
```

## By whom? von or durch

Most passive sentences never say who did it. When they do, there are two prepositions:

- **{von}** + dative names the **doer**, usually a person or an organisation: {Das Café wurde von einem Konditor gegründet.} {Der Brand wurde von der Feuerwehr gelöscht.}
- **{durch}** + accusative names the **means**, the cause or the go-between: {Ich wurde durch einen lauten Knall geweckt.} {Viele Krankheiten werden durch Viren übertragen.}

A quick test: if you could say "by means of" or "through" in English, {durch} is likely. With natural events both are used: {Die Stadt wurde durch ein Erdbeben zerstört} and {von einem Erdbeben} are both correct.

:::tip
{von dem} contracts to {vom}, as always: {Das Brot wird vom Bäcker gebacken.}
:::

```words
durch | through; by (means of)
das Erdbeben, - | earthquake
der Brand, ¨-e | fire, blaze
die Feuerwehr, -en | fire brigade
löschen | to put out (a fire); to delete
```

## With modal verbs: muss repariert werden

{Das Glas muss nach Farben getrennt werden.} The glass has to be separated by colour.

With a modal verb, the passive infinitive goes to the end: **participle + {werden}**. The modal verb itself is conjugated as usual:

- {Batterien dürfen nicht in den Hausmüll geworfen werden.}
- {Pfandflaschen können im Supermarkt zurückgegeben werden.}
- past: {Die Heizung musste repariert werden.}
- subordinate clause, modal last: {…, weil die Heizung repariert werden muss.}

```words
der Müll (Sg.) | rubbish, waste
die Tonne, -n | bin; tonne
trennen | to separate
recyceln | to recycle
weg|werfen (wirft weg, warf weg, hat weggeworfen) | to throw away
die Verpackung, -en | packaging
das Pfand (Sg.) | deposit (on bottles)
zurück|geben (gibt zurück, gab zurück, hat zurückgegeben) | to give back, to return
die Umwelt (Sg.) | environment
```

## Hier wird nicht geraucht: the impersonal passive

German can make even verbs without an object passive. The result has **no subject at all**, and describes an activity going on:

- {Hier wird nicht geraucht.} There's no smoking here.
- {Am Samstag wird gefeiert!} On Saturday we're celebrating!
- {Sonntags wird nicht gearbeitet.} Nobody works on Sundays.

The verb is always in the third person singular ({wird}, {wurde}). If nothing else stands in first position, {es} fills the gap, but it disappears as soon as something else comes first:

- {Es wird getanzt.} → {Im Saal wird getanzt.} (not {Im Saal es wird getanzt})

Parents also use it as a very firm command: {Jetzt wird geschlafen!} Bedtime. Now.

:::mistake
Dative verbs such as {helfen}, {danken} or {gratulieren} have no accusative object to promote, so their passive has no subject either, and the person stays in the dative: {Mir wurde sofort geholfen.} I was helped straight away. **Not** {Ich wurde geholfen}: that is one of the most frequent mistakes English speakers make.
:::

```words
rauchen | to smoke
```

## When German prefers the passive

Use the passive when the doer is **unknown**, **obvious** or **unimportant**, and the action is what counts:

| Where | Example |
| --- | --- |
| processes and recipes | {Die Zwiebeln werden klein geschnitten.} |
| rules and notices | {Hunde müssen an der Leine geführt werden.} |
| news | {In Basel ist am Montag ein neues Museum eröffnet worden.} |
| history | {Die Berliner Mauer wurde 1961 gebaut.} |

In conversation, {man} often does the same job more lightly: {Hier trennt man den Müll} says much the same as {Hier wird der Müll getrennt}. And when the doer matters, use the active: {Meine Schwester hat das Brot gebacken} is better than {Das Brot ist von meiner Schwester gebacken worden}, unless you want to put the spotlight on the bread.

```words
die Heizung, -en | heating; radiator
der Techniker, - | technician, engineer
```
