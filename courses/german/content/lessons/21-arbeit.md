{Was machen Sie beruflich?} is one of the first questions Germans ask a new acquaintance, often before "Where are you from?". Work matters here: {Beruf} is related to {Berufung}, a calling. This lesson gives you the words for jobs and workplaces, takes you through a job advert, an application and a phone call to a hotel in Salzburg, and adds three pieces of grammar you need to talk about a working life: **als** and **wenn** for "when", the **Präteritum** of the verbs every story uses, and the **future** with {werden}.

One warning for returners before we start: {ich will} does **not** mean "I will". It means "I want". "I will" is {ich werde}. If that sentence alone sticks, the lesson has paid for itself.

## Was machst du beruflich?

Hannah and Luca meet at a friend's birthday party in Bern.

```dialogue
title: Auf einer Party in Bern
Hannah: Und, was machst du beruflich, Luca? | So, what do you do for a living, Luca?
Luca: Ich bin Koch. Ich arbeite in einem italienischen Restaurant in der Altstadt. | I'm a chef. I work in an Italian restaurant in the old town.
Hannah: Ach, wie schön! Arbeitest du Vollzeit? | Oh, how lovely! Do you work full-time?
Luca: Ja, leider auch am Wochenende. Und du? | Yes, unfortunately at weekends too. And you?
Hannah: Ich bin Grafikdesignerin. Ich arbeite Teilzeit für eine kleine Firma und bin daneben selbstständig. | I'm a graphic designer. I work part-time for a small company and I'm self-employed on the side.
Luca: Und wie gefällt dir die Arbeit? | And how do you like the work?
Hannah: Sehr gut! Meine Kollegen sind nett, und ich kann oft im Homeoffice arbeiten. | Very much! My colleagues are nice, and I can often work from home.
Luca: Das kann ich als Koch leider nicht. Aber dafür gibt's bei mir immer gutes Essen! | As a chef I can't do that, unfortunately. But on the other hand there's always good food where I work!
```

Remember from A1 that a job after {sein} has **no article**: {Ich bin Koch}, {Sie ist Ingenieurin}. With {arbeiten} you use **als**: {Ich arbeite als Koch.} And to ask, either {Was machen Sie beruflich?} or {Was sind Sie von Beruf?}

:::fun
{Homeoffice} is German, not English: an English speaker "works from home". German has a whole family of these home-made anglicisms: {das Handy} (mobile phone), {der Beamer} (projector), {der Oldtimer} (vintage car), {das Public Viewing} (watching football on a big screen outdoors, which in English sounds like a funeral).
:::

:::culture
Germany, Austria and Switzerland are famous for the **{Ausbildung}**: a two- or three-year apprenticeship that combines work in a company with classes at a vocational school. Bankers, chefs, mechanics, shop assistants and IT specialists all train this way, and for many young people it is a real alternative to university. A {Studium} is a university degree; {studieren} is only used for that, never for school. At school you {lernen}.
:::

## Am Telefon

Business calls follow a script, and knowing it takes most of the fear out of them.

| the caller says | the company says |
| --- | --- |
| {Guten Tag, mein Name ist …} | {Hotel Mozartblick, Gruber am Apparat.} (… speaking) |
| {Kann ich bitte Frau Leitner sprechen?} | {Einen Moment, bitte. Ich verbinde.} (I'll put you through.) |
| {Ich rufe wegen der Stellenanzeige an.} | {Frau Leitner ist gerade in einer Besprechung.} |
| {Kann sie mich zurückrufen?} | {Kann ich etwas ausrichten?} (Can I take a message?) |
| {Vielen Dank, auf Wiederhören!} | {Gern geschehen. Auf Wiederhören!} |

:::note
To ask for a man, say {Kann ich bitte **Herrn** Wagner sprechen?} {Herr} adds an -n in every case except the nominative. You will meet more nouns like this at B1.
:::

```words
verbinden (verbindet, verband, hat verbunden) | to connect; to put through
zurück|rufen (ruft zurück, rief zurück, hat zurückgerufen) | to call back
aus|richten | to pass on (a message)
die Besprechung, -en | meeting
erreichen | to reach
am Apparat | speaking (on the phone)
```

## Als oder wenn?

English has one "when"; German has three, and the difference is not optional:

| | use it for | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **{als}** | **one** occasion or period in the past | {Als ich 17 war, hatte ich meinen ersten Job.} |
| **{wenn}** | the present and future; and "whenever" (repeated) in the past | {Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an.} {Immer wenn mein Chef kam, war er schlecht gelaunt.} |
| **{wann}** | questions, direct or indirect | {Wann beginnt das Gespräch?} {Ich weiß nicht, wann es beginnt.} |

{Als} and {wenn} both send the verb to the end, just like {weil}. A period of time counts as one occasion, even if things happened often within it: {Als ich in Wien wohnte, ging ich jeden Tag ins Café.}

:::mistake
**{Wenn ich ein Kind war, …}** is one of the commonest mistakes in German. Childhood happened once, so it is **{Als ich ein Kind war, …}** If you could add "every time" or "whenever" in English, use {wenn}; for a single past event or period, {als}.
:::

## Es war einmal: the Präteritum

In conversation Germans talk about the past mostly in the perfect ({ich bin gegangen}, {ich habe gesagt}). But a few very common verbs are nearly always used in the **Präteritum**, the simple past, even in speech: {war}, {hatte}, the modal verbs ({konnte}, {musste}) and {es gab}. And in writing (stories, reports, the news) the Präteritum is the normal past tense. You need to recognise its commonest forms now; B1 covers the rest.

| infinitive | Präteritum | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {sein} | {war} | was |
| {haben} | {hatte} | had |
| {werden} | {wurde} | became |
| {geben} | {gab} ({es gab}) | gave (there was, there were) |
| {gehen} | {ging} | went |
| {kommen} | {kam} | came |
| {sagen} | {sagte} | said |
| {arbeiten} | {arbeitete} | worked |

Regular verbs add **-te** to the stem ({sagte}, {machte}, and {arbeitete} with an extra e after t). Irregular verbs change their vowel instead and add no -te ({ging}, {kam}, {gab}). Either way, the endings are simple, and **ich** and **er/sie/es** are the same, with no ending at all:

| | {gehen} | {sagen} |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | {ging} | {sagte} |
| du | {gingst} | {sagtest} |
| er/sie/es | {ging} | {sagte} |
| wir | {gingen} | {sagten} |
| ihr | {gingt} | {sagtet} |
| sie/Sie | {gingen} | {sagten} |

```words
es gab | there was, there were
werden (wird, wurde, ist geworden) | to become; will (with an infinitive)
```

## Ich werde …: the future

The future tense is {werden} + an **infinitive at the end**, the same bracket as with modal verbs:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {ich werde … arbeiten} | {wir werden … arbeiten} | |
| {du wirst … arbeiten} | {ihr werdet … arbeiten} | |
| {er/sie/es wird … arbeiten} | {sie/Sie werden … arbeiten} | |

German uses it less than English uses "will". For plans with a time word, the **present** is normal: {Nächstes Jahr arbeite ich in Wien.} The future with {werden} is for:

- **predictions**: {Morgen wird es regnen.} {Du wirst die Stelle bestimmt bekommen!}
- **promises and firm intentions**: {Ich werde pünktlich sein.} {Ich werde dich anrufen.}
- **plans, when you want to sound definite**: {Ich werde nächstes Jahr in Zürich studieren.}

In a subordinate clause, {werden} goes to the end like any conjugated verb: {Ich verspreche, dass ich pünktlich sein werde.}

:::mistake
**{ich will}** = I want. **{ich werde}** = I will. {Ich will Lehrerin werden} means "I want to become a teacher". And keep three look-alikes apart: {ich werde} (I will / I become), {ich wurde} (I became), and {ich würde} (I would, B1).
:::

{Werden} on its own means "to become": {Es wird dunkel} (it's getting dark), {Ich werde 30} (I'm turning 30), {Sie wird Ärztin} (she's going to be a doctor). Only with an infinitive does it make the future.

::conjugator{verb="werden"}
