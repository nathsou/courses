At A2 you learnt to give reasons with {weil} and {denn}. At B1 the difference between sounding like a textbook and sounding like a person is in the joints: *still*, *otherwise*, *not only … but also*, *the longer …, the more …*. German has a word for each, and each one does something specific to the verb.

That is the part returners tend to have half-forgotten: some linking words leave the verb alone, some send it to the end, and some pull it forward. This lesson sorts them into three families, so you know what to do with the verb the moment you choose the word. The theme is a question many people in Munich, Vienna and Zurich ask themselves when they look at their rent: {Stadt oder Land?}

## Stadt oder Land?

```dialogue
title: Ein Haus im Allgäu
Hannah: Mehmet, schau mal! Ein Haus im Allgäu, mit Garten und Blick auf die Berge. Und die Miete ist niedriger als hier. | Mehmet, look! A house in the Allgäu, with a garden and a view of the mountains. And the rent is lower than here.
Mehmet: Klingt schön. Aber wie kommen wir dann zur Arbeit? Entweder wir pendeln jeden Tag zwei Stunden, oder wir brauchen ein Auto. | Sounds lovely. But how would we get to work? Either we commute two hours every day or we need a car.
Hannah: Ich kann drei Tage pro Woche im Homeoffice arbeiten. Deshalb wäre das für mich kein Problem. | I can work from home three days a week. So that wouldn't be a problem for me.
Mehmet: Und für mich? Im Dorf gibt es weder ein Kino noch eine Kneipe. | And for me? There's neither a cinema nor a pub in the village.
Hannah: Dafür gibt es nicht nur Ruhe, sondern auch frische Luft. Und je länger ich in München wohne, desto mehr nervt mich der Lärm. | But there's not only peace and quiet but fresh air too. And the longer I live in Munich, the more the noise gets on my nerves.
Mehmet: Das verstehe ich. Trotzdem würde ich meine Freunde vermissen. | I understand that. Still, I'd miss my friends.
Hannah: Deine Freunde wären dann zwar weiter weg, aber sie könnten uns besuchen. Wir hätten ja ein Gästezimmer! | Your friends would be further away, true, but they could visit us. We'd have a guest room, after all!
Mehmet: Na gut. Schauen wir es uns am Wochenende an, sonst denkst du ja an nichts anderes mehr. | All right. Let's go and look at it at the weekend, otherwise you won't think about anything else.
```

## Three families of linking words

The same idea can be joined in three ways, and the linking word decides where the verb goes:

| Family | Linking word | The verb… | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | {und, aber, oder, denn, sondern} | stays where it was | {Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde.} |
| 2 | {weil, dass, obwohl, wenn, sodass…} | goes to the end | {Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich müde bin.} |
| 3 | {deshalb, trotzdem, außerdem, sonst…} | comes straight after it | {Ich bin müde, deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.} |

Family 1 are the conjunctions that sit **outside** the sentence, in "position zero": they join two ordinary main clauses and change nothing. German schoolchildren remember them as **ADUSO**: {aber, denn, und, sondern, oder}. Family 2 are the subordinating conjunctions from A2. Family 3 are not conjunctions at all but **adverbs**: they take up the first position of the main clause themselves, so the verb, which must be second, comes immediately after them, and the subject moves behind it.

:::key
Family 3 words can also stand in the middle of the clause instead, with normal word order before them: {Ich bin müde, ich bleibe deshalb zu Hause.} Both are correct. What is never correct is {deshalb ich bleibe}: if the adverb comes first, the verb follows it.
:::

## Deshalb, trotzdem, außerdem, sonst, also

```words
deshalb | therefore, that's why
deswegen | that's why, so
trotzdem | still, nevertheless
außerdem | besides, what's more
sonst | otherwise; else
also | so, therefore
```

- **{deshalb}** and **{deswegen}** (and the slightly more emphatic {darum}) introduce a **result**: {Die Mieten sind hoch, deshalb ziehen viele Familien aufs Land.} Make sure the reason comes first: {deshalb} looks forward to the consequence, while {weil} and {denn} look back to the cause.
- **{trotzdem}**: something happens although you would not expect it. {Das Haus ist alt, trotzdem gefällt es uns.}
- **{außerdem}** adds another point: {Die Wohnung ist zu teuer, außerdem ist sie zu laut.}
- **{sonst}**: "otherwise", "or else". {Beeil dich, sonst verpassen wir den Zug!}
- **{also}**: "so", drawing a conclusion. {Der Bus ist weg, also gehen wir zu Fuß.}

:::mistake
**{also} is a false friend.** It does not mean "also" (that is {auch}). {Ich komme also mit} means "So I'm coming along", not "I'm also coming". And after {und}, an adverb still pulls the verb forward: {Es regnet, und deshalb bleiben wir zu Hause.}
:::

## Obwohl oder trotzdem?

{obwohl} and {trotzdem} express the same idea, an unexpected result, but they belong to different families. {obwohl} introduces the *surprising circumstance* and sends its verb to the end. {trotzdem} introduces the *unexpected result* and pulls its verb forward:

- {Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren.} Although it's raining, we're going for a walk.
- {Es regnet. Trotzdem gehen wir spazieren.} It's raining. We're going for a walk anyway.
- {Trotz des Regens gehen wir spazieren.} (the genitive version from the last lesson)

Here is the whole pattern for reasons and concessions:

| | cause | unexpected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| conjunction (verb at the end) | {weil} | {obwohl} |
| adverb (verb straight after) | {deshalb} (points to the result) | {trotzdem} |
| preposition (+ genitive) | {wegen} | {trotz} |

```words
obwohl | although
```

:::mistake
{Trotzdem es regnet …} is a mixture of the two and is not standard German (you may hear it in some dialects). Use {Obwohl es regnet, …}. And {trotzdem} comes in the clause with the *result*: {Es regnet, trotzdem gehen wir}, not {Trotzdem es regnet, gehen wir}.
:::

## Nicht nur …, sondern auch

Some connectors come in two parts, one before each of the things they join. They sound polished, and B1 exams love them.

| Connector | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {sowohl … als auch} | both … and | {Sowohl Hannah als auch Mehmet arbeiten in München.} |
| {nicht nur …, sondern auch} | not only … but also | {Das Dorf ist nicht nur ruhig, sondern auch günstig.} |
| {entweder … oder} | either … or | {Entweder wir pendeln, oder wir kaufen ein Auto.} |
| {weder … noch} | neither … nor | {Im Dorf gibt es weder ein Kino noch eine Kneipe.} |
| {zwar …, aber} | admittedly … but; … true, but | {Die Wohnung ist zwar klein, aber sie ist günstig.} |

A few details worth getting right:

- **Commas**: always before {sondern} and {aber}. None with {sowohl … als auch} and {weder … noch}. With {entweder … oder}, none between words and phrases; between two whole clauses the comma is optional.
- **The verb**: {sowohl … als auch} makes a plural subject ({Sowohl Hannah als auch Mehmet arbeiten …}). With {weder … noch}, singular and plural are both used.
- **{entweder}** can stand outside the clause ({Entweder wir pendeln …}) or take the first position itself ({Entweder pendeln wir …}). Both are correct.
- **{zwar}** sits in the middle of its clause, usually after the verb: {Die Wohnung ist zwar klein, aber …} It concedes a point before you make your real one, which makes it the most useful word in any discussion.

```words
sowohl … als auch | both … and
nicht nur … sondern auch | not only … but also
entweder … oder | either … or
weder … noch | neither … nor
zwar … aber | admittedly … but
```

## Je länger, desto besser

"The longer …, the more …" is {je …, desto …}. The {je} part is a subordinate clause, so its verb goes to the end; the {desto} part is the main clause, with the comparative first and then the verb:

- {Je länger ich in München wohne, desto mehr nervt mich der Lärm.}
- {Je mehr du übst, desto leichter wird es.}
- {Je älter man wird, umso schneller vergeht die Zeit.} ({umso} is a common alternative to {desto})

Both halves start with a comparative, and nothing comes between {desto} and its comparative. Without verbs it makes neat short phrases: {Je früher, desto besser.} The sooner the better. {Je mehr, desto lustiger.} The more the merrier.

```words
je … desto | the … the (je länger, desto besser)
```

## Sodass, so … dass, während

Three more family-2 conjunctions, so the verb goes to the end:

- **{sodass}** (also written {so dass}) introduces a **consequence**: {Der Zug hatte Verspätung, sodass ich den Anschluss verpasst habe.}
- **{so}** + adjective … **{dass}** puts the emphasis on the adjective: {Der Zug war so voll, dass ich stehen musste.}
- **{während}** can mean "while" in time (from lesson 24), but also "whereas", setting two facts against each other: {Während Hannah das Land liebt, ist Mehmet ein Stadtmensch.}

:::grammar[Three kinds of während]
{während} + genitive is a preposition: {während des Films} (during the film). {während} + clause in time: {Während ich koche, kannst du den Tisch decken.} (while). And {während} + clause in contrast: {In der Stadt gibt es viele Kinos, während es im Dorf keins gibt.} (whereas). In both clause uses, the verb goes to the end.
:::

```words
sodass | so that (with the result that)
```
