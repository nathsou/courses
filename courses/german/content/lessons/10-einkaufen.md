---
title: Shopping and clothes
goals:
  - name clothes and sizes, and ask to try something on in a shop
  - point things out with dieser (this one) and ask welcher? (which one?)
  - say what you like, what fits and what suits you with gefällt mir, passt mir, steht dir
  - buy food in the right quantities, from ein Kilo to eine Flasche

Shopping is a good workout for the German you already have: numbers and prices (lesson 3), the accusative ({Ich nehme den Pullover}, lesson 5), {ihn}, {sie} and {es} ({Ich nehme ihn}, lesson 7), and the polite {Können Sie …?} and {Ich möchte …} from lesson 9.

This lesson adds three things. First, {dieser} and {welcher}, "this one" and "which one", whose endings you can already predict. Second, a small group of verbs where German turns English logic upside down: you do not like the jumper, **the jumper pleases you**. And third, how to ask for things by the kilo, the bottle and the packet without ever saying "of".

## Die Kleidung

```words
die Kleidung (Sg.) | clothes, clothing
das Hemd, -en | shirt
die Bluse, -n | blouse
das T-Shirt, -s | T-shirt
der Pullover, - | jumper, sweater
die Hose, -n | (pair of) trousers
die Jeans, - | (pair of) jeans
der Rock, ¨-e | skirt
das Kleid, -er | dress
die Jacke, -n | jacket
der Mantel, ¨ | coat
der Schuh, -e | shoe
die Mütze, -n | woolly hat, cap
tragen (trägt, trug, hat getragen) | to wear; to carry
```

Notice:

- **{die Hose}** is singular. English "trousers" and "jeans" are plural; German says {eine Hose}, {eine Jeans}: {Die Hose ist zu lang.} The plural {Hosen} means several pairs.
- **{tragen}** changes its vowel like {fahren}: {du trägst}, {er trägt}. {Was trägst du heute?} What are you wearing today?
- Everyday German shortens {der Pullover} to {der Pulli}. In Austria a woolly hat is {die Haube}.

Colours (lesson 7) work after {sein} as usual: {Der Rock ist rot.} After {in}, a colour becomes a noun and takes a capital letter: {Haben Sie den auch in Blau?} Do you have it in blue too?

| --- | --- |
| {Kann ich Ihnen helfen?} | {Ich suche einen Pullover.} / {Nein, danke, ich schaue nur.} |
| {Welche Größe haben Sie?} | {Ich habe Größe 38.} / {Größe M.} |

{eine Nummer größer} (one size bigger) and {eine Nummer kleiner} are fixed phrases; comparatives come at A2.

```dialogue
title: Haben Sie den auch in Blau?
Verkäuferin: Grüß Gott! Kann ich Ihnen helfen? | Hello! Can I help you?
Luca: Ja, gern. Ich suche einen Pullover. | Yes, please. I'm looking for a jumper.
Verkäuferin: Welche Größe haben Sie? | What size are you?
Luca: Größe M. | Size M.
Verkäuferin: Wie finden Sie diesen hier? Der ist gerade im Angebot. | What do you think of this one? It's on offer at the moment.
Luca: Hm, die Farbe gefällt mir nicht so. Haben Sie den auch in Blau? | Hm, I don't really like the colour. Do you have it in blue too?
Verkäuferin: Ja, hier. Möchten Sie ihn anprobieren? | Yes, here. Would you like to try it on?
Luca: Gern. Wo sind die Kabinen? | Yes, please. Where are the changing rooms?
Verkäuferin: Dort hinten links. | Back there on the left.
Verkäuferin: Und? Passt er? | Well? Does it fit?
Luca: Er ist leider ein bisschen zu eng. Haben Sie ihn eine Nummer größer? | Unfortunately it's a bit too tight. Do you have it a size bigger?
Verkäuferin: Moment … Ja, hier ist L. | One moment … Yes, here's an L.
Luca: Perfekt, der passt. Ich nehme ihn. | Perfect, that one fits. I'll take it.
```

The pullover is {der Pullover}, so it is {er} and {ihn} all the way through: {Passt er?}, {Ich nehme ihn}. And the shop assistant says {der} and {den} where English says "it" or "that one": {Der ist gerade im Angebot.} {Haben Sie den auch in Blau?} In speech, {der}, {die}, {das} are very often used like this, as a stronger "it".

:::mistake
**"Too" has two translations.** "Too" meaning *excessively* is {zu}: {Der Mantel ist zu teuer.} "Too" meaning *also* is {auch}: {Die Jacke ist auch schön.} And {sehr} (very) is not a complaint: {Der Mantel ist sehr teuer} just states a fact; {zu teuer} means you are not buying it.
:::

:::culture
Clothes sizes are European: women's sizes run 34, 36, 38, 40 and so on (a UK 10 is roughly a German 38), men's jackets 46, 48, 50, and shoes 36 to 46 (a UK 8 is about a 42). :::

## Dieser und welcher

{dieser} means "this" (and often "that": German rarely makes the distinction), and {welcher} means "which". Their endings copy the endings of the definite article:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | {der} → {dieser} | {die} → {diese} | {das} → {dieses} | {die} → {diese} |
| accusative | {den} → {diesen} | {die} → {diese} | {das} → {dieses} | {die} → {diese} |
| which? | {welcher} / {welchen} | {welche} | {welches} | {welche} |

The rule: **look at the last letter of the article** and put it on {dies-} or {welch-}. {der} gives -er, {den} gives -en, {die} gives -e, {das} gives -es. As always, only the masculine changes in the accusative.

- {Dieser Pullover ist schön.} {Ich nehme diesen Pullover.}
- {Welcher Rock gefällt dir?} {Welchen Rock nimmst du?}
- {Welches Kleid nimmst du, dieses oder das da?}

::article-table{kind=demonstrative cases=nom,acc}

```words
dieser | this, that
welcher | which
```

:::tip
In conversation people often point with {der}, {die}, {das} plus {hier} or {da}, instead of {dieser}: {Welchen nehmen Sie?} — {Den hier.} {Welche Schuhe?} — {Die da.} You need {dieser} for writing and careful speech; {der hier} for the shop floor.
:::

## Gefällt dir das?

To say you like how something looks, German uses {gefallen}, and it works back to front:

- {Der Pullover gefällt mir.} I like the jumper. (literally: the jumper pleases me)
- {Die Schuhe gefallen mir.} I like the shoes. (the shoes please me)
- {Gefällt dir das Kleid?} Do you like the dress?
- {Wie gefällt Ihnen die Jacke?} How do you like the jacket?

The thing you like is the **subject**, so the verb agrees with the thing: one jumper {gefällt}, two shoes {gefallen}. The person who likes it is in the **dative**: {mir} (me), {dir} (you), {Ihnen} (you, formal). The dative gets a whole lesson at A2; for now, these three words are enough for any shop.

| | ich | du | Sie |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accusative (me, you) | {mich} | {dich} | {Sie} |
| dative | {mir} | {dir} | {Ihnen} |

Two more verbs work the same way:

- **{passen}**, to fit: {Die Hose passt mir nicht.} The trousers don't fit me. It also means "to suit, be convenient": {Passt dir Samstag?} Does Saturday suit you?
- **{stehen}**, to suit, look good on: {Die Farbe steht dir gut!} That colour suits you! {Rot steht mir nicht.} Red doesn't suit me.

And one useful phrase: {Das ist mir zu teuer.} That's too expensive for me.

```words
gefallen (gefällt, gefiel, hat gefallen) (+ D) | to please; Das gefällt mir: I like it
passen (+ D) | to fit; to suit
stehen (steht, stand, hat gestanden) (+ D) | to suit (someone); to stand
mir | (to) me
dir | (to) you
Ihnen | (to) you (formal)
```

:::mistake
"I like the jumper" is **not** {Ich gefalle …}: {ich gefalle} means "I please", and {Ich gefalle dem Pullover} would mean that the jumper likes *you*. Turn the sentence round: {Der Pullover gefällt mir.} And {gefallen} vs {mögen}: {gefallen} is mostly about how something looks or a first impression; {mögen} (lesson 5) is general, lasting liking: {Ich mag Käse.}
:::

## Im Supermarkt

```words
das Kilo, -s | kilo
das Gramm, -e | gram
der Liter, - | litre
die Flasche, -n | bottle
die Packung, -en | packet, pack
die Dose, -n | tin, can
das Stück, -e | piece
die Tüte, -n | (carrier) bag
das Pfand (Sg.) | deposit (on bottles)
```

German quantities have **no "of"**: the amount goes straight in front of the food.

- {ein Kilo Äpfel} a kilo of apples
- {eine Flasche Wasser} a bottle of water
- {200 Gramm Käse} 200 grams of cheese
- {eine Packung Nudeln} a packet of pasta
- {ein Stück Butter} a pack of butter (a 250 g block)

After a number, masculine and neuter measures **stay singular**: {zwei Kilo}, {500 Gramm}, {drei Liter}, {zwei Stück Kuchen}. Feminine ones take the plural: {zwei Flaschen}, {drei Packungen}, {zwei Dosen}. In Germany you will also hear {ein Pfund} (500 grams): {ein Pfund Tomaten}.

At the cheese or meat counter the conversation always runs the same way: {Was darf's sein?} (What can I get you?) — {Ich hätte gern 200 Gramm Gouda.} — {Darf's ein bisschen mehr sein?} (Is a little over OK?) — {Ja, gern.} — {Sonst noch etwas?} (Anything else?) — {Nein, danke, das ist alles.}

:::culture
In Germany most drinks bottles and cans carry a deposit, {das Pfand}, of up to 25 cents. You get it back by feeding the empties into a machine in the supermarket, which prints a voucher for the till. Carrier bags cost money everywhere, so people bring their own: the question at the till is {Brauchen Sie eine Tüte?} In Austria the bag is {das Sackerl}, in Switzerland {das Säckli}. And at an Austrian deli counter you order in {Deka} (10 grams): {zwanzig Deka Schinken} is 200 grams of ham.
:::
