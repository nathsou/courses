Every Monday morning, in offices, classrooms and kitchens from Hamburg to Zurich, someone asks {Was hast du am Wochenende gemacht?} To answer, you need the **perfect tense**, {das Perfekt}, the normal way to talk about the past in spoken German.

If you learnt German before, you may remember the perfect as "the present perfect", the English *I have done*. It is not. {Ich habe Tennis gespielt} means "I played tennis", "I have played tennis" and "I was playing tennis", depending on the context. In conversation it is simply *the* past tense. German also has a simple past ({ich spielte}), but in speech it is used mainly for a handful of verbs, above all {sein} and {haben}: {ich war}, {ich hatte}. You will learn both in this lesson.

## Was hast du gemacht?

```words
das Wochenende, -n | weekend
gestern | yesterday
vorgestern | the day before yesterday
schon | already
machen | to do, to make
kaufen | to buy
feiern | to celebrate
putzen | to clean
der Geburtstag, -e | birthday
```

The perfect has two parts: a form of **{haben}** in the verb slot, and the **past participle** at the end. It is the same bracket you met with the modal verbs in lesson 9:

| Position 1 | Verb | Middle | End |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| {Ich} | {habe} | {am Samstag Fußball} | {gespielt.} |
| {Am Abend} | {haben} | {wir Lenas Geburtstag} | {gefeiert.} |
| {Was} | {hast} | {du am Wochenende} | {gemacht?} |
| | {Hast} | {du etwas} | {gekauft?} |

{haben} is conjugated as usual ({ich habe}, {du hast}, {er hat} …); the participle never changes.

For **regular verbs** the participle is easy: **ge-** + stem + **-t**.

| Infinitive | Stem | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {machen} | mach | {gemacht} |
| {kaufen} | kauf | {gekauft} |
| {spielen} | spiel | {gespielt} |
| {hören} | hör | {gehört} |
| {tanzen} | tanz | {getanzt} |
| {arbeiten} | arbeit | {gearbeitet} |

If the stem ends in -t or -d, add **-et** so that you can hear the ending: {gearbeitet}, {gekostet}. And verbs in -ieren take no ge-: {fotografieren} → {fotografiert}. Lesson 13 has the remaining patterns.

## Gegessen, getrunken, gesehen

The common irregular (strong) verbs make their participle with **ge-** + stem + **-en**, and the stem often changes its vowel. There is no reliable way to predict the vowel, so learn the participle with the verb, as the word lists do: {schreiben (schreibt, schrieb, hat geschrieben)}.

```words
essen (isst, aß, hat gegessen) | to eat
trinken (trinkt, trank, hat getrunken) | to drink
sehen (sieht, sah, hat gesehen) | to see
schreiben (schreibt, schrieb, hat geschrieben) | to write
schlafen (schläft, schlief, hat geschlafen) | to sleep
das Buch, ¨-er | book
der Brief, -e | letter
die Nachricht, -en | message; die Nachrichten: the news
der Krimi, -s | crime novel, crime drama
```

| Infinitive | Participle | | Infinitive | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| {essen} | {gegessen} | | {schreiben} | {geschrieben} |
| {trinken} | {getrunken} | | {schlafen} | {geschlafen} |
| {sehen} | {gesehen} | | {treffen} | {getroffen} |
| {lesen} | {gelesen} | | {singen} | {gesungen} |

## Ich bin gefahren: perfect with sein

Some verbs make their perfect with **{sein}** instead of {haben}. At this level the rule is simple: **verbs of movement from one place to another** take {sein}.

- {Wir sind nach Wien gefahren.} We went to Vienna.
- {Ich bin ins Kino gegangen.} I went to the cinema.
- {Wann bist du nach Hause gekommen?} When did you get home?
- {Wir sind am Sonntag gewandert.} We went hiking on Sunday.

Plus two verbs you simply have to remember: **{bleiben}** (to stay, which is the opposite of movement!) and **{sein}** itself:

- {Ich bin zu Hause geblieben.} I stayed at home.
- {Ich bin in Berlin gewesen.} I have been / was in Berlin.

```words
kommen (kommt, kam, ist gekommen) | to come
bleiben (bleibt, blieb, ist geblieben) | to stay
sein (ist, war, ist gewesen) | to be
der Ausflug, ¨-e | trip, outing
das Museum, Museen | museum
der Berg, -e | mountain
die Ausstellung, -en | exhibition
der Flohmarkt, ¨-e | flea market
```

The movement verbs from lesson 9 also take {sein}: {fahren}, {gehen}, {schwimmen}, {wandern}. That is why their word-list entries read {ist gefahren}, {ist gegangen}, {ist geschwommen}: the principal parts always tell you which one a verb takes.

:::mistake
English always uses "have", so learners say "Ich habe nach Hause gegangen". Check the verb: does it take you from A to B? Then it is {sein}: {Ich bin nach Hause gegangen.} The participle is the same either way; only the helping verb changes.
:::

## War und hatte

For {sein} and {haben}, spoken German mostly uses the simple past, the {Präteritum}, instead of the perfect. {Ich war im Kino} is far more common than {Ich bin im Kino gewesen}, and {Ich hatte keine Zeit} more common than {Ich habe keine Zeit gehabt}.

| | {sein} | {haben} |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {ich} | {war} | {hatte} |
| {du} | {warst} | {hattest} |
| {er/sie/es} | {war} | {hatte} |
| {wir} | {waren} | {hatten} |
| {ihr} | {wart} | {hattet} |
| {sie/Sie} | {waren} | {hatten} |

As with the modal verbs, the {ich} and {er} forms are the same.

```words
das Wetter (Sg.) | weather
langweilig | boring
toll | great
müde | tired
krank | ill
```

:::culture
In southern Germany, Austria and Switzerland the simple past has almost vanished from speech: you will hear {Ich bin krank gewesen} where a Hamburger says {Ich war krank}. The south also uses {sein} with {stehen}, {sitzen} and {liegen}: {Ich bin gestanden} (standard: {Ich habe gestanden}). Both are correct German; this course uses the standard forms.
:::

## Gestern, letzte Woche, vor zwei Tagen

```words
letzte Woche | last week
vor zwei Tagen | two days ago
noch nie | never (yet), never before
```

| | |
| --- | --- |
| {gestern}, {gestern Abend} | yesterday, yesterday evening |
| {vorgestern} | the day before yesterday |
| {am Wochenende}, {am Samstag} | at the weekend, on Saturday |
| {letzten Montag} | last Monday |
| {letzte Woche} | last week |
| {letztes Jahr} | last year |
| {vor zwei Tagen}, {vor einer Woche}, {vor einem Jahr} | two days ago, a week ago, a year ago |

The endings of {letzt-} copy the article, exactly like {dieser} in lesson 10: {der Montag} → {letzten Montag} (accusative, because it says *when*), {die Woche} → {letzte Woche}, {das Jahr} → {letztes Jahr}. And "ago" is {vor}, placed **before** the time: {vor zwei Tagen}. Take those forms as phrases for now; the dative comes at A2.

A time word very often comes first, and then the verb is second, as always: {Gestern habe ich lange geschlafen.} {Letzte Woche war ich in Graz.}

And for experiences: {Ich habe noch nie Sushi gegessen.} I've never eaten sushi. {Warst du schon mal in Zürich?} Have you ever been to Zurich?
