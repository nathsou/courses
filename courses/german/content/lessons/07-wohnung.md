The German-speaking countries are countries of tenants. About half of all households in Germany rent their home, in Berlin more than four in five, and Switzerland has the lowest rate of home ownership in Europe. So flat-hunting, adverts full of abbreviations and the famous **WG** (a flat shared by people who are not a couple or a family) are part of everyday life, and a favourite topic of conversation.

In this lesson you show people round your home, say what you think of things, and read a real-style flat advert. The grammar builds directly on lesson 5: once you know that {der} becomes {den}, you are ready for {er} becoming {ihn}.

## Die Wohnung

```words
das Zimmer, - | room
die Küche, -n | kitchen
das Bad, ¨-er | bathroom
das Schlafzimmer, - | bedroom
das Wohnzimmer, - | living room
der Flur, -e | hall, corridor
der Balkon, -s | balcony
der Garten, ¨ | garden
```

Rooms are a good place to see how German builds words: {schlafen} + {das Zimmer} = {das Schlafzimmer}, {wohnen} + {das Zimmer} = {das Wohnzimmer}, {das Kind} + {das Zimmer} = {das Kinderzimmer}. The **last** part always decides the gender, so every kind of {Zimmer} is {das}. {das Bad} is short for {das Badezimmer}; on signs and in adverts the toilet is often just {das WC}.

:::culture
In Austria the hall is {das Vorzimmer}. All over the region the toilet is often a small room of its own, separate from the bathroom, so adverts may list {Bad} and {WC} separately. Very handy in a WG.
:::

```match
title: Which room?
pairs:
  - [die Küche, Hier koche ich.]
  - [das Schlafzimmer, Hier schlafe ich.]
  - [das Bad, Hier dusche ich.]
  - [das Wohnzimmer, Hier sehe ich fern.]
  - [der Flur, Hier ist die Tür.]
  - [der Balkon, Hier trinke ich Kaffee in der Sonne.]
```

## Möbel: furniture

```words
der Stuhl, ¨-e | chair
das Sofa, -s | sofa
der Sessel, - | armchair
das Bett, -en | bed
der Schrank, ¨-e | cupboard, wardrobe
das Regal, -e | shelf, set of shelves
der Kühlschrank, ¨-e | fridge
der Herd, -e | cooker, stove
das Fenster, - | window
die Tür, -en | door
```

{die Möbel} (furniture) is a plural word in German: {Die Möbel sind neu.} One piece of furniture is {ein Möbelstück}.

:::culture
In Austria a wardrobe is usually {der Kasten}, and {der Sessel} is any chair: an Austrian armchair is {der Fauteuil}. And in Germany, don't be surprised by an "unfurnished" flat with no kitchen at all: tenants often bring their own fitted kitchen, cooker and fridge included, and take it with them when they move. Adverts mention it when one is included (more on that below).
:::


## Groß, klein, gemütlich: describing things

```words
groß | big, large; tall
klein | small, little
hell | bright, light
dunkel | dark
neu | new
alt | old
schön | beautiful, lovely
gemütlich | cosy, comfortable
teuer | expensive
billig | cheap
ruhig | quiet
laut | loud, noisy
```

After {sein}, an adjective is simply added, with **no ending**, whatever the gender or number:

- {Die Küche ist klein.} The kitchen is small.
- {Der Balkon ist groß.} The balcony is big.
- {Das Sofa ist neu.} The sofa is new.
- {Die Zimmer sind hell.} The rooms are bright.

:::mistake
If you learnt German before, you may remember adjective endings and add one here: {Die Küche ist kleine} is wrong. Endings appear only when the adjective stands **before** a noun ({die kleine Küche}), and that is the subject of lesson 19. After {sein}: no ending, ever.
:::

{gemütlich} is a word Germans are proud of. It means cosy, comfortable, relaxed and welcoming all at once: a room, a café or an evening can be {gemütlich}. {billig} means cheap, sometimes in the sense of "cheaply made"; in adverts you will read the friendlier {günstig} (good value).

```words
weiß | white
schwarz | black
rot | red
blau | blue
grün | green
gelb | yellow
grau | grey
braun | brown
```

For lighter and darker shades, add {hell-} or {dunkel-}: {hellblau} (light blue), {dunkelgrün} (dark green). To ask about colour: {Welche Farbe hat das Sofa?} – {Es ist grau.}


## Es gibt: there is, there are

```words
es gibt | there is, there are
```

To say what a place has or what exists, German uses {es gibt}, literally "it gives". Two things to know:

1. What follows is in the **accusative**, because it is the object of {gibt}: {Es gibt einen Balkon.} {Es gibt keinen Garten.}
2. It is always {gibt}, singular, even before a plural: {Es gibt zwei Schlafzimmer.}

Questions start with the verb, and the verb-second rule applies as always:

- {Gibt es hier einen Supermarkt?} Is there a supermarket here?
- {In der Küche gibt es einen Herd.} In the kitchen there is a cooker.
- {Hier gibt es keinen Aufzug.} There's no lift here.

:::mistake
{Es gibt ein Balkon} is the classic slip: {der Balkon} is masculine and the object of {gibt}, so {Es gibt einen Balkon}. The same goes for {keinen}: {Es gibt keinen Kühlschrank!}
:::

```cloze
title: What's in the flat?
prompt: Type the missing word.
items:
  - de: Es gibt [einen] Balkon, aber [keinen] Garten.
    en: There's a balcony, but no garden.

    en: There are three rooms in the flat.
    explain: Always singular {gibt}, even with a plural.
  - de: Im Wohnzimmer gibt es [ein] Sofa und [einen] Sessel.
    en: In the living room there's a sofa and an armchair.
  - de: '[Gibt](geben) es hier [einen] Supermarkt?'
    en: Is there a supermarket here?
  - de: Es gibt [eine] Küche, [ein] Bad und zwei Schlafzimmer.
    en: There's a kitchen, a bathroom and two bedrooms.
  - de: In der Küche gibt es [keinen] Kühlschrank!
    en: There's no fridge in the kitchen!
```

## Wie findest du …? Ich finde ihn schön.

```words
finden (findet, fand, hat gefunden) | to find; to think (of)
```

{finden} means "to find", and also "to think of": {Wie findest du die Wohnung?} (What do you think of the flat?) – {Ich finde sie toll.} (I think it's great.) Its stem ends in -d, so it adds an -e- like {arbeiten}: {du findest}, {er findet}.

```dialogue
title: Die neue Wohnung
Aylin: Komm rein! Das ist meine neue Wohnung. | Come in! This is my new flat.
Mehmet: Wow, die Wohnung ist aber hell! | Wow, the flat is really bright!
Aylin: Ja, es gibt viele Fenster. Und hier ist das Wohnzimmer. | Yes, there are lots of windows. And here's the living room.
Mehmet: Ist das Sofa neu? | Is the sofa new?
Aylin: Nein, es ist alt, von meiner Oma. Wie findest du es? | No, it's old, from my grandma. What do you think of it?
Mehmet: Ich finde es sehr gemütlich. Und der Teppich ist toll! | I think it's very cosy. And the rug is great!
Aylin: Der Teppich? Ich finde ihn ein bisschen hässlich. Aber er ist von meinem Bruder. | The rug? I think it's a bit ugly. But it's from my brother.
Mehmet: Gibt es auch einen Balkon? | Is there a balcony too?
Aylin: Leider nein. Aber die Küche ist groß, und es gibt einen Garten! | Unfortunately not. But the kitchen is big, and there's a garden!
```

Look at {Wie findest du es?} (the sofa) and {Ich finde ihn hässlich} (the rug). When a pronoun replaces a direct object, it goes into the accusative too, and once again only the masculine changes: {er} becomes **{ihn}**, just as {der} becomes {den}.

| nominative | {ich} | {du} | {er} | {sie} | {es} | {wir} | {ihr} | {sie} | {Sie} |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accusative | {mich} | {dich} | **{ihn}** | {sie} | {es} | {uns} | {euch} | {sie} | {Sie} |

::article-table{kind=personal cases="nom,acc"}

:::grammar[It is not always es]
English says "it" for every thing. German uses the pronoun of the noun's gender (lesson 4), so: {der Schrank} → {Ich finde ihn schön.} {die Lampe} → {Ich finde sie schön.} {das Bett} → {Ich finde es schön.} Plural {die Stühle} → {Ich finde sie schön.} Saying {es} for everything is the most persistent habit of English speakers; listen for {ihn} and you will start hearing it everywhere.
:::

## Where does nicht go?

Lesson 5 settled {nicht} or {kein}. Now: where does {nicht} go? Three rules cover almost everything at this level.

**1. To negate the whole sentence, {nicht} goes towards the end**: after the object and after time words.

- {Ich mag den Teppich nicht.} I don't like the rug.
- {Wir kochen heute nicht.} We're not cooking today.

**2. But it goes before the words that complete the verb**: an adjective after {sein} or {finden}, a place, a separable prefix, and {gern}.

- {Die Küche ist nicht groß.} The kitchen isn't big.
- {Ich finde ihn nicht schön.} I don't think it's nice.
- {Wir wohnen nicht in Wien.} We don't live in Vienna.
- {Ich stehe heute nicht auf.} I'm not getting up today.

**3. For a contrast, it goes right before the word you are contradicting**, usually with {sondern} (but rather): {Ich finde nicht die Küche schön, sondern das Bad.}

:::tip
A useful picture: {nicht} moves as far back as it can, but stops in front of the words that belong closely to the verb. If the verb has a prefix or an adjective at the end, {nicht} goes just before it.
:::

```order
title: Put nicht in its place
items:
  - en: The kitchen isn't big.
    de: Die Küche ist nicht groß.
    also: [Groß ist die Küche nicht.]
  - en: I don't like the rug.
    de: Ich mag den Teppich nicht.
    also: [Den Teppich mag ich nicht.]
    extra: [kein]
  - en: We don't live in Vienna.
    de: Wir wohnen nicht in_Wien.
    also: [In_Wien wohnen wir nicht.]
  - en: He isn't getting up today.
    de: Er steht heute nicht auf.
    also: [Heute steht er nicht auf.]
  - en: I don't think it's nice. (the lamp)
    de: Ich finde sie nicht schön.
    also: [Schön finde ich sie nicht.]
```

## Wohnungssuche: reading a flat advert

```words
die Miete, -n | rent
die Nebenkosten (Pl.) | service charges, bills on top of the rent
die WG, -s | shared flat
der Quadratmeter, - | square metre
```

{WG} stands for {Wohngemeinschaft}, "living community": a flat shared by friends or strangers, each with their own room and a shared kitchen and bathroom. Students, young workers and plenty of people in their thirties live in one. Adverts are short and full of abbreviations:

| Abbreviation | Meaning | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zi. | {Zimmer} | rooms |
| qm, m² | {Quadratmeter} | square metres |
| EBK | {Einbauküche} | fitted kitchen (included!) |
| NK | {Nebenkosten} | service charges |
| kalt / warm | {Kaltmiete} / {Warmmiete} | rent without / with heating and bills |
| EG, 1. OG | {Erdgeschoss}, {erstes Obergeschoss} | ground floor, first floor |
| ab sofort | | available now |

:::culture
In Germany a {3-Zimmer-Wohnung} has three rooms **plus** kitchen and bathroom: typically a living room and two bedrooms. Switzerland counts half rooms: in a {3½-Zimmer-Wohnung} the half is usually a dining area or a large hall. In Austria a small one-room flat is a {Garçonnière}. Landlords usually ask for a deposit ({die Kaution}), in Germany up to three months' rent.
:::

```read

setting: Two adverts on a flat-hunting website, one in Leipzig and one in Vienna.
```
